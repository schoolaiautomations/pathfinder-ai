CIRCLE 1

Do you really want it?

Many students want the microphone, fame or television screen — not the daily reporting work behind it. Tap your honest answer.

Can you approach strangers, ask clear questions and keep asking follow-ups when the first answer is vague?

Do you enjoy reading, writing and rewriting until a complicated story becomes simple and accurate?

Can you delay publishing an exciting claim until you verify it properly, even when others are posting it first?

Can you handle corrections, rejected story ideas, editor criticism and public disagreement without giving up?

Are you willing to follow current affairs every day — politics, courts, business, science, local issues and public policy — instead of only topics you personally like?

Can you work around breaking news, travel for field reporting, record video/audio and sometimes work evenings, weekends or holidays?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & skills

What to focus on in school, and which road to take after Class 10.

1English
Reading speed, grammar, vocabulary, interviewing and clear writing matter every day. Strong English also opens national and digital-media opportunities.
2Telugu
For journalism in Andhra Pradesh, strong spoken and written Telugu is a major advantage. Learn to explain serious issues in natural, simple Telugu — not only formal textbook language.
3Social Studies & Current Affairs
History, civics, economics, geography and daily news help you understand what a story means instead of only repeating what someone said.
After Class 10
Intermediate CEC / Humanities is the most relevant route, but MPC, BiPC or any recognized stream is also accepted. After Class 12, you can join BA Journalism / Mass Communication / BJMC. Admission may be through CUET-UG, a college-specific entrance test, or Class 12 merit depending on the institution.

THE LONGER ROAD — QUALIFICATIONS YOU WILL NEED LATER
After Class 12
You can choose BA Journalism / Mass Communication, BJMC, BAMMC, BA Media Studies or a related undergraduate programme. Eligibility varies by college, but these programmes commonly accept students from multiple Class 12 streams.
Is a journalism degree compulsory?
No single professional licence is required to work as a journalist in India. Employers may hire graduates in Journalism, Communications, English or other fields if their reporting, writing and portfolio are strong. Subject expertise — for example economics, science, law or technology — can also become a reporting advantage.
Postgraduate route
After a bachelor's degree, specialised journalism and media programmes are another option. IIMC currently offers postgraduate journalism, radio/TV, digital-media and related programmes. A postgraduate qualification can help, but it still does not replace a strong portfolio.
Portfolio you need
Build 8–12 pieces that prove you can actually report: a local news story, interview, profile, fact-check, data story, short video report, photo story and one deeper explanatory piece. Show your sources and corrections clearly.
Tools to learn
Phone/video recording, audio, basic editing, photography, headline writing, CMS publishing, social media, search, spreadsheets, simple data visualisation and fact-checking tools. Modern journalists are increasingly expected to work across formats.
Professional rules
Accuracy, verification, fairness, source handling, privacy, copyright and legal awareness matter. The Press Council of India specifically emphasises checking factual accuracy and cross-checking information before publication.
You must clear
Class 10 → any suitable Intermediate stream → bachelor's degree / journalism programme → portfolio + internships → reporter / sub-editor / digital journalist / video journalist → specialised beat or senior editorial role
Do firstBefore paying for a journalism course, report one real story yourself. Pick a local issue, speak to at least three people, verify the facts, write 500 words and make a 60-second video version. If you enjoy the reporting process — not just presenting it — this career deserves a closer look.
CIRCLE 3 · THE RISK

How often are the jobs?

Journalism exists everywhere, but direct newsroom jobs are more competitive and fragmented than the number of YouTube channels makes them look.

Who hires
Newspapers, television news channels, digital newsrooms, magazines, news agencies, radio, specialised business/technology publications and local-language media. In Andhra Pradesh, current openings appear in cities such as Visakhapatnam and Vijayawada, while larger media markets include Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi and Mumbai.
How often
Openings appear throughout the year, but many are internships, contract roles, freelance assignments or digital-content positions rather than permanent “Reporter” jobs. Current Andhra Pradesh listings include journalism internships and sub-editor roles, showing that entry paths can be mixed rather than one standard graduate recruitment cycle.
What employers now expect
Writing alone is often not enough. Newsrooms increasingly ask for research, fact-checking, social media, CMS use, basic photo/video skills, scripting and audience understanding. Telugu + English + multimedia ability makes you much more employable in this region.
AI changes the job
Routine summaries, transcription and basic rewriting are becoming easier to automate. Your stronger long-term value is original reporting, trusted sources, field access, verification, judgement, interviews, investigation and subject expertise — work that cannot be replaced by simply generating text.
CarefulA journalism degree by itself does not guarantee a newsroom job. If you graduate with no published work, no reporting experience and no multimedia ability, you will compete against candidates who already have clips, internships, YouTube packages, newsletters or local reporting experience.

CIRCLE 4

What you will earn

Journalism can become a respected and influential career, but the first few years are often paid much less than students expect.

Intern / trainee
Rs 0 – 15,000/month
Some internships are unpaid or offer small stipends. Current Indian listings include journalism internships around Rs 7,000–7,500/month.
Starting reporter / journalist
Rs 15,000 – 30,000/month
Indeed currently reports an India-wide journalist average base salary of about Rs 17,205/month. Strong digital, English-language or metro roles can start higher.
Mid-level (3–7 years)
Rs 30,000 – 70,000/month
Reporter, correspondent, producer, sub-editor or specialist beat journalist. Pay varies sharply by organisation, city, language, portfolio and audience reach.
Senior / specialist
Rs 60,000 – 1,50,000+/month
Senior correspondents, editors, producers and recognised specialists can earn more. Famous anchors and national personalities are exceptions — do not use celebrity salaries when planning this career.
The cost before that
Journalism education has no single national price. A local public-college degree can cost far less than a private media school. As a later postgraduate benchmark, IIMC's current published fee is about Rs 95,500 for PG Diploma in Journalism (English), Rs 1.685 lakh for Radio & TV Journalism and Rs 2.085 lakh for Digital Media. These are postgraduate fees, not Class-12 undergraduate fees.
Ask at homeDoes it make sense to spend several lakhs on a private media degree if the likely first salary is Rs 15,000–30,000/month? Compare course fees, internships, alumni outcomes and newsroom access. In this field, a lower-cost degree plus excellent reporting work can sometimes be smarter than an expensive degree with no portfolio.

How to read your four circles

All four right — go ahead with full commitment, starting today, not after results.

Any one wrong — stop and think twice before you decide.

Two or more wrong — you are choosing this career for the wrong reasons. Talk to your counsellor.

For journalism, add one extra test: would you still want this career if nobody recognised your face and your first job was behind the scenes? If the answer is no, you may want broadcasting fame more than journalism itself.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

If the four circles came out right, start reporting now. You do not need to wait for college to test whether this work suits you.

1Right now, in school
  • Read one Telugu and one English news source every day and compare how they report the same event
  • Write a 150-word summary of one local issue each week using only verified facts
  • Interview a teacher, shopkeeper, farmer, student, athlete or local worker and turn the conversation into a short profile
2After Class 10
  • Choose the Intermediate stream you can perform well in; CEC/Humanities is useful but not compulsory
  • Improve Telugu + English writing, speaking, typing, basic photography, mobile video and editing
  • Create a simple student-news page, newsletter or YouTube series and publish one properly verified story every two weeks
3After Class 12
  • Shortlist your target colleges first, then prepare for the correct admission route: CUET-UG where required, university-specific entrance/aptitude tests where applicable, or merit-based admission. Do not prepare for an exam until you confirm that your target programme actually uses it
  • Compare journalism/mass-communication degrees with other bachelor's routes; check fees, faculty, student publications, studio access, internships and alumni outcomes — not just advertisements
  • By the end of first year, build at least 8–12 strong published clips across text, interview, video, photo, fact-check and data formats
  • Apply early for internships with local newspapers, Telugu newsrooms, digital publications, radio, magazines and national media; be willing to move to a stronger media market if necessary
Best first testThis weekend, report one real local story. Three interviews + two independent sources + 500 words + one 60-second video. That single exercise will tell you far more about your fit for journalism than watching news anchors on television.

This is career guidance, not an official admission, employment or salary notice. Course eligibility, fees, newsroom requirements, vacancies and pay change over time. Journalism also involves legal, ethical and safety responsibilities.
