Speech-Language Pathologist

Speech-Language Pathologists help children and adults who have difficulty with speech, language, communication, voice, fluency, feeding or swallowing. In India, the main professional undergraduate route is B.ASLP — Bachelor in Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology, followed by registration in the Central Rehabilitation Register (CRR) under the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI).

Would Speech-Language Pathology suit you?

This career needs patience, science, observation and communication. Progress can be slow, so you must enjoy helping someone improve step by step.

Do you enjoy understanding how people speak, listen, communicate and learn language?

Can you patiently work with a child or adult who may need the same skill practised many times?

CIRCLE 2

Which pathway can you take?

The professional route is clearer than many students think: choose Science after Class 10, complete an RCI-recognised B.ASLP programme, finish the internship and obtain the required professional registration.

Important termsB.ASLP = Bachelor in Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology. RCI = Rehabilitation Council of India. CRR = Central Rehabilitation Register. RCI recognises professional rehabilitation qualifications and maintains the CRR for registered professionals.
After Class 10
Choose the Science stream.

Under the current RCI B.ASLP curriculum, Class 12 applicants must have studied any two or more subjects in the science stream and meet the prescribed minimum marks.

BiPC is the most directly relevant choice for students interested in speech, hearing, anatomy and rehabilitation. MPC/other science combinations may also qualify when they meet the college and RCI rules.
B.ASLP eligibility
The current RCI B.ASLP curriculum specifies 10+2 or equivalent with at least 50% marks, with category relaxations according to applicable government/university rules, and at least two science subjects in the qualifying examination.

Individual universities may have additional admission conditions, so always check the college prospectus.
Main professional degree
B.ASLP — 4 academic years including 1 year of internship.

The programme combines Audiology + Speech-Language Pathology. Students learn both hearing and communication sciences before graduating as professionals trained across the two closely connected fields.
What you study
Typical subjects include Anatomy & Physiology, Linguistics, Psychology, Speech & Language Development, Speech Disorders, Voice, Fluency, Audiology, Hearing Assessment, Hearing Aids, Neurology, Clinical Practice and Rehabilitation.
Admission exam
There is no single compulsory national B.ASLP entrance exam for every college in India.

Admission may be through university/state counselling, entrance examination or Class 12 merit depending on the institution. First shortlist RCI-recognised B.ASLP colleges, then check each institution's current admission route.
Choose the college carefully
RCI recognition is essential. Before paying fees, verify that the institution and B.ASLP programme are currently recognised by RCI for your academic session.

A college website saying “Speech Therapy course available” is not enough.
After graduation
Complete the B.ASLP + internship → apply for registration in the Central Rehabilitation Register (CRR) as applicable → work as an Audiologist / Speech-Language Pathology professional within the recognised scope.

RCI lists Audiologists and Speech Therapists among the categories of rehabilitation professionals registered in the CRR.
Higher study
M.Sc. Speech-Language Pathology (M.Sc. SLP) is a 2-year postgraduate route under the current RCI curriculum.

The current RCI eligibility requires a recognised B.ASLP / B.Sc. Speech & Hearing degree with the prescribed aggregate marks. Postgraduate study can support advanced clinical, teaching and research roles.
You must clear
Class 10 → Science in Class 11–12 → admission to an RCI-recognised B.ASLP → 4 years including 1-year internship → CRR registration → Speech-Language Pathologist / Audiologist-SLP role → optional M.Sc. SLP for advanced specialisation.
Very importantDo not join an unrecognised “speech therapy” certificate and assume it makes you a Speech-Language Pathologist. For professional rehabilitation practice, recognised qualification and CRR registration are central requirements under the RCI framework.
CIRCLE 3 · CAREER AREAS

Who does a Speech-Language Pathologist help?

SLPs work across the lifespan — from infants learning to feed and communicate to adults recovering speech after stroke or brain injury.

Child Language Disorders
Help children who have delayed or disordered understanding and use of language. Work may involve vocabulary, grammar, comprehension, conversation and communication with family/school.
ChildrenLanguageCommunication
Speech Sound Disorders
Assess and treat difficulty producing speech sounds clearly, including articulation and phonological disorders.
Autism Communication Support
Support functional communication, language, social communication and alternative/augmentative communication when appropriate as part of a multidisciplinary team.
Stuttering / Fluency
Assess and manage fluency disorders such as stuttering, including communication strategies and counselling components within professional scope.
Voice Disorders
Work with people who have voice problems related to vocal misuse, medical conditions or surgery, often in coordination with ENT specialists.
Adult Neurogenic Communication
Help people with aphasia, dysarthria, apraxia and cognitive-communication problems after stroke, traumatic brain injury or neurological disease.
Feeding & Swallowing
Trained SLPs may assess and manage feeding/swallowing difficulties within their competence and clinical scope, often working closely with doctors and other healthcare professionals.
AAC Specialist
AAC = Augmentative and Alternative Communication. Use picture systems, communication boards, devices or software to support people who cannot rely on speech alone.
Cleft Lip / Palate & Resonance
Work with speech and resonance difficulties related to structural conditions, typically as part of a multidisciplinary cleft/craniofacial team.
Hearing-related Communication
Because B.ASLP includes Audiology, professionals understand how hearing loss affects speech/language and can work in rehabilitation alongside hearing assessment and amplification services.
School-based SLP
Support children whose communication difficulties affect classroom participation, learning and social interaction, where such services are available.
Hospital / Rehabilitation SLP
Work with ENT, Neurology, Pediatrics, Rehabilitation Medicine and other departments on communication, voice and swallowing cases.
SLP vs Audiologist
Speech-Language Pathologist: focuses mainly on speech, language, voice, fluency, communication and swallowing.
Audiologist: focuses mainly on hearing and balance assessment/rehabilitation.

B.ASLP trains students in both areas, though professionals may later specialise.
SLP vs Special Educator
SLP: assesses and treats communication/speech-language disorders.
Special Educator: focuses on teaching and educational support for learners with disabilities.

They often work together but follow different professional training routes.
SLP vs Psychologist
SLP: communication, speech, language, voice, fluency and swallowing.
Psychologist: cognition, behaviour, emotion and psychological assessment/intervention according to their professional qualification.

A child may need both professionals.
Where you can work
Hospitals, ENT clinics, rehabilitation centres, child-development centres, schools, hearing/speech clinics, early-intervention centres, NGOs, universities, research centres and private practice subject to registration and applicable rules.
AI-era relevance
AI can assist with speech analysis, transcription, AAC, therapy materials and remote monitoring, but human clinical judgement, relationship-building, differential assessment and individualised therapy remain central. Future SLPs who understand digital tools and assistive communication technology may have stronger options.

Career realityTherapy progress can take time. A good SLP does not simply ask a child to repeat words; they assess why communication is difficult, choose appropriate goals, measure progress and involve the family when needed.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Salary varies with city, experience, setting, specialisation and whether you work in a hospital, child-development centre, school, private clinic or your own practice.

Intern / trainee
Stipend varies
B.ASLP includes a full internship year. Stipend arrangements differ by institution, so check the college before admission.
Starting SLP
₹20,000 – ₹35,000/month
A broad practical early-career range for many clinics, hospitals and child-development centres. Location and caseload can change this significantly.
India benchmark
≈ ₹35,952/month
Indeed's February 2026 India benchmark for Speech-Language Pathologist was about ₹35,952/month, based on 25 reported salaries. This mixes experience levels and should not be treated as a guaranteed fresher salary.
Experienced SLP
₹35,000 – ₹60,000+/month
Specialised paediatric, neurological, voice, swallowing or AAC experience can improve opportunities. Current job listings also show some experienced positions above this range.
Private practice
Highly variable
Income depends on location, session fees, caseload, clinic expenses, referral network, reputation and professional registration. Revenue is not the same as profit.
Course-cost warning
B.ASLP fees vary substantially by institution. Before paying, verify RCI recognition for the exact academic year, clinical facilities, internship exposure, faculty and patient caseload. An expensive unrecognised programme can create serious professional problems later.
Money warningDo not choose a college only because it promises placement or says “speech therapy.” RCI recognition, strong supervised clinical training and eligibility for CRR registration are more important than marketing.

How to read your four circles

Language + healthcare + patient work — Speech-Language Pathology may be a strong fit.

Love hearing technology more than speech therapy — explore Audiology.

Love teaching children more than clinical assessment — compare Special Education.

One extra rule: a Speech-Language Pathologist helps people communicate better — but the work is clinical science, not simply speaking practice.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, first test whether you enjoy both the science of communication and patient-facing work.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen Biology, English and basic Psychology/human-development awareness
  • Observe how children learn sounds, words and sentences at different ages
  • Practise patient listening and clear communication rather than only speaking fast or confidently
2After Class 10
  • Choose a Science stream; BiPC is the most directly relevant route
  • Shortlist only RCI-recognised B.ASLP programmes
  • Check each college's current entrance/merit requirements rather than assuming one national exam
3During B.ASLP
  • Take both Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology seriously before specialising
  • Build supervised clinical experience with children and adults
  • After internship, complete the applicable CRR registration and consider M.Sc. SLP if you want advanced clinical/research work
Best first testObserve a qualified Speech-Language Pathologist for a day if a clinic allows student observation. Notice that therapy is not simply talking to someone: the clinician assesses the problem, chooses a goal, uses structured activities and records the response. If that science + communication + patient progress combination interests you, SLP may genuinely suit you.
