Environmental Consultant

Environmental Consultants help industries, infrastructure projects, governments and organisations understand and reduce their impact on air, water, land, ecosystems and communities. The work can combine science, engineering, field surveys, data, laws, reports and client advice. It is not only “protecting nature” — much of the job is measuring impacts, solving pollution problems and helping projects meet environmental rules.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want environmental consulting?

Caring about the environment is a good start. But consultants also need to analyse data, understand regulations, write reports and sometimes tell clients uncomfortable facts.

Do you enjoy Science and want to understand problems such as air pollution, water pollution, waste, climate impacts or ecosystem damage?

Would you enjoy collecting field data, visiting industrial/project sites and working outdoors as well as in an office?

Can you work carefully with numbers, maps, laboratory results, standards and technical reports?

Can you explain environmental risks clearly to engineers, managers, government officials and local communities?

Are you willing to learn environmental laws and compliance requirements instead of doing only nature-related fieldwork?

Can you stay evidence-based even when a client wants a faster or cheaper answer?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

There is no single degree called “Environmental Consultant.” People enter through science, engineering, ecology, geology and related fields.

1Biology / Ecology
Useful for biodiversity, ecosystems, wildlife, ecological surveys, restoration and environmental-impact work.
2Chemistry
Important for water quality, air pollution, soil contamination, hazardous waste and laboratory/environmental monitoring.
3Maths / Data
Consultants use statistics, modelling, spreadsheets, GIS, monitoring data and calculations to support decisions.
After Class 10
MPC is the strongest route if you want Environmental Engineering, Civil Engineering, Chemical Engineering, water/wastewater or technical pollution-control work.

BiPC is strong for Environmental Science, Ecology, Biology, biodiversity, public-health/environment and conservation-oriented work.

If you are unsure, choose the science combination that best matches the environmental problems you want to solve.
Science route
Class 12 Science → B.Sc. Environmental Science / Environmental Studies / Ecology / Geology / Chemistry / Life Sciences → M.Sc. Environmental Science / Ecology / Environmental Management or another specialist master's → environmental consulting / research / compliance roles.
Engineering route
MPC → B.Tech/B.E. in Environmental, Civil, Chemical or related Engineering → environmental / water / waste / pollution-control role → M.Tech or specialist experience where useful.

Environmental Engineering is not offered at every undergraduate college, so Civil or Chemical Engineering followed by environmental specialisation is also a common route.
Entrance exams
There is no national entrance exam for “Environmental Consultant.”

AP EAPCET / JEE — relevant if you choose an engineering route in participating colleges.

CUET-UG — some participating universities admit Environmental Science or related science programmes through CUET-UG.

Other universities may use Class 12 merit or their own process.
Useful higher study
Depending on your speciality, useful postgraduate routes include M.Sc. Environmental Science, Environmental Management, Ecology, Climate Science, Geoinformatics/GIS, M.Tech Environmental Engineering, Water Resources, Waste Management or related programmes.
Professional / regulatory reality
Environmental Consultant is not one single licensed profession in India. However, formal Environmental Impact Assessment work has additional requirements. India's environmental-clearance system lists QCI/NABET-accredited EIA Consultant Organisations, and projects requiring environmental clearance are processed through the government's PARIVESH system under applicable environmental rules.
You must clear
Class 10 → MPC or BiPC → relevant B.Sc./B.Tech degree → internships + field/data/regulatory skills → Environmental Analyst / Engineer / EIA / Sustainability role → Environmental Consultant.
A Master's degree is especially useful for science-heavy, EIA, ecology, climate and specialist consulting roles.
ImportantDo not choose a degree only because it contains the word “environment.” Check whether it teaches practical skills such as sampling, laboratory analysis, GIS, EIA, pollution control, environmental law, statistics and fieldwork.
CIRCLE 3 · CAREER AREAS

What kind of Environmental Consultant could you become?

Environmental consulting includes many very different types of work. Your degree and technical skills determine where you fit.

EIA Consultant
Environmental Impact Assessment. Study how a proposed mine, factory, highway, power plant, port, building or other project could affect air, water, land, ecology and communities, and recommend mitigation measures.
EIAEMPClearances
Environmental Compliance
Help industries understand and meet environmental-clearance conditions, pollution-control consents, waste rules, monitoring requirements and reporting obligations.
Air Quality
Measure and model air pollutants, evaluate industrial emissions, interpret monitoring data and recommend pollution-control systems.
Water & Wastewater
Work on water quality, sewage treatment, industrial effluent, ETP/STP design or performance, groundwater and water-management studies.
ETPSTPWater Quality
Waste Management
Municipal solid waste, hazardous waste, biomedical waste, e-waste, plastics, recycling and circular-economy projects. Work can include audits, facility planning, compliance and waste-reduction programmes.
Contaminated Land / Remediation
Investigate soil and groundwater contamination from industrial activity, spills or waste disposal and help design clean-up/remediation plans.
Ecology / Biodiversity
Conduct habitat, vegetation, wildlife and biodiversity surveys; assess ecological impacts; and support conservation or restoration plans. Biology/ecology backgrounds are especially useful here.
GIS / Remote Sensing
Use maps, satellite imagery, drones and geospatial data to analyse land use, forests, water bodies, project impacts and environmental change.
Climate / Carbon
Greenhouse-gas inventories, carbon footprints, climate-risk assessments, decarbonisation strategies and climate-adaptation studies.
ESG / Sustainability
Help companies measure and improve environmental performance across energy, water, emissions, waste, supply chains and sustainability disclosures. This is more business/reporting-oriented than traditional field ecology.
Environmental Audit
Review a facility's environmental systems, permits, emissions, waste handling and compliance performance; identify gaps and recommend corrective action.
Noise / Occupational Environment
Measure noise and other environmental conditions around industries, transport and infrastructure and recommend controls.
Environmental Modelling
Use computer models for air dispersion, water systems, hydrology, floods, climate, noise or other environmental processes. Strong Maths, coding and GIS skills help.
Consultant vs Environmental Scientist
Environmental Scientist: focuses strongly on scientific measurement, analysis and research.
Environmental Consultant: applies science/engineering to a client's project, regulation or business problem.
Many people move between the two.
Consultant vs Environmental Engineer
Environmental Engineer: often designs technical systems such as treatment plants, pollution controls and infrastructure solutions.
Environmental Consultant: may work more broadly across impact assessment, compliance, audits, monitoring, ecology or sustainability.
An Environmental Engineer can also become a consultant.
Government regulation
Projects covered by India's environmental-clearance framework may be processed through PARIVESH, which tracks Environment, Forest, Wildlife and CRZ clearances. The portal also publishes the EIA Notification framework and lists accredited EIA consultants.
Andhra Pradesh context
Industries in Andhra Pradesh may need environmental approvals and AP Pollution Control Board permissions such as Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate under applicable Water/Air laws. Environmental professionals help projects prepare data, monitoring reports and compliance documentation — but the regulator, not the consultant, grants the approval.
Who hires
Environmental consultancies, engineering/EPC firms, infrastructure companies, mining, manufacturing, chemicals, pharma, power, renewable energy, real estate, waste companies, water companies, ESG/sustainability teams, NGOs and research organisations.
Your Plan B
Environmental skills can also lead into:

Ethics ruleA consultant's job is not to “make pollution disappear on paper.” Good consulting measures impacts honestly, recommends workable controls and helps projects comply with environmental requirements.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Environmental consulting pay depends heavily on whether you are a fresh science graduate, engineer, GIS specialist, EIA expert, sustainability consultant or senior project manager.

Intern / trainee
₹8,000 – ₹20,000/month
Internships can involve sampling, data entry, field surveys, GIS, laboratory coordination or report preparation. Quality of experience matters more than title.
Entry-level
₹20,000 – ₹35,000/month
A broad starting range for junior environmental analysts, consultants and engineers. Technical engineering/GIS skills can improve the starting position.
India benchmark
≈ ₹32,100/month average
Indeed's February 2026 Environmental Consultant benchmark is about ₹32,102/month. It is based on only 2 reported salaries, so treat it as a rough reference rather than a guaranteed market salary.
Experienced consultant
₹40,000 – ₹80,000+/month
Consultants with strong EIA, regulatory, modelling, water, GIS, climate or project-management experience can move higher.
Senior / specialist
₹70,000 – ₹1,50,000+/month
Senior consultants, project managers, NABET/EIA experts, sustainability leads and specialised engineers may earn more depending on company, sector and responsibility.
Independent consulting
Highly variable
Experienced professionals can consult independently, but regulatory credibility, professional network, technical team, laboratory/GIS support and client acquisition all matter. Formal EIA work may require working within the applicable accredited-organisation framework.
Study cost
Public B.Sc./B.Tech programmes can be relatively affordable, while private engineering or postgraduate programmes can cost several lakhs. Compare the cost with laboratory quality, internships, GIS/software training, placements and industry exposure.
Career warningEnvironmental consulting is a strong niche career, but a general degree without practical skills can make job hunting difficult. Build at least one hard skill: GIS, water/wastewater, EIA, air modelling, laboratory analysis, data/coding or environmental compliance.

How to read your four circles

Science + problem-solving + real-world impact — Environmental Consulting may fit you.

Love wildlife more than industry/regulation — compare Ecology, Wildlife Biology or Conservation.

Love designing treatment systems — Environmental / Civil / Chemical Engineering may be the better primary identity.

One extra rule: environmental consulting is where science meets projects, laws and business decisions.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

A school student does not need to know environmental law yet. Start by learning to measure environmental problems rather than only talking about them.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen Science, Maths and Geography
  • Pick one local issue — waste, water, air pollution or flooding — and collect real observations/data
  • Learn basic spreadsheets, graphs and mapping
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC for engineering-heavy environmental careers or BiPC for environmental science/ecology-heavy careers
  • Shortlist B.Sc. Environmental Science or B.Tech routes such as Environmental/Civil/Chemical Engineering
  • Check whether your target institution uses CUET-UG, AP EAPCET, JEE, merit or another admission process
3During college
  • Learn GIS/QGIS, Excel/data analysis, sampling, environmental law and technical report writing
  • Get internships with consultancies, industries, water/waste companies, NGOs or environmental laboratories
  • If you want EIA work, learn the EIA Notification, PARIVESH process and QCI/NABET consultant framework
Best first testChoose a local water body, drain or waste hotspot. Map the area, photograph it safely, identify possible pollution sources and write a one-page report with problem → evidence → likely cause → possible solution. If you enjoy turning an environmental problem into evidence and practical recommendations, this career may genuinely suit you.
