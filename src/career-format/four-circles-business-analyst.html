Business Analyst

A Business Analyst understands a business problem, talks to users and managers, studies processes and data, and helps teams decide what should change. In many companies, Business Analysts work between business teams and technology teams — translating needs into clear requirements, process improvements, reports, dashboards or product changes.

CIRCLE 1

Would Business Analysis suit you?

A Business Analyst is not simply someone who makes PowerPoint presentations. The real job is understanding problems clearly, asking good questions and helping people agree on a workable solution.

Do you enjoy asking “Why is this happening?” and finding the real cause of a problem?

Would you enjoy talking to users, managers and technical teams to understand what each group needs?

Can you organise confusing information into a clear step-by-step process?

Are you comfortable with numbers, spreadsheets, charts and basic data analysis?

Can you explain a problem simply to someone who does not have your background?

Would you be comfortable learning both business concepts and basic technology/tools?

CIRCLE 2

Which pathway can you take?

There is no single compulsory Business Analyst degree or national entrance exam. People enter this career from Business, Commerce, Economics, Engineering, Computer Science, Statistics and many other backgrounds.

Useful termsBA = Business Analyst. BRD = Business Requirements Document. UAT = User Acceptance Testing. SQL = Structured Query Language, used to work with data in databases. BPMN = Business Process Model and Notation, a standard way to draw business processes.
After Class 10
You can choose any stream.

MEC / Commerce is useful if you enjoy business, economics and numbers.

MPC is useful if you may later choose Engineering, Computer Science, Statistics or technical analytics.

CEC / Humanities can also work if you build strong quantitative, Excel and technology skills later.
Route A — BBA / B.Com
Class 12 → BBA / B.Com → Excel + business processes + basic SQL + Power BI → internship → Junior Business Analyst / Operations Analyst.

This route is strong for students who like business, finance, operations and communication.
Route B — B.Tech / B.E.
MPC → Engineering in CSE / IT / ECE / other branch → understand software systems + SQL + requirements + business processes → IT Business Analyst / Product Analyst.

This is a common route in technology companies because the student understands both systems and business needs.
Route C — Economics / Statistics
Class 12 → BA/B.Sc. Economics, Statistics or Mathematics → Excel + SQL + dashboards + business understanding → Business Analyst / Data-oriented BA / Strategy Analyst.

This route is especially useful for students who enjoy analytical problem-solving.
Route D — Computer Applications / IT
Class 12 → BCA / B.Sc. Computer Science / IT → SQL + systems + process mapping + communication → IT Business Analyst / Functional Analyst.
MBA route
Bachelor's degree → CAT / XAT / CMAT / other business-school route → MBA/PGDM → Business Analyst / Strategy / Consulting / Product / Operations roles.

An MBA can help for management-oriented or consulting roles, but it is not compulsory for every Business Analyst job.
Optional certifications
Later, depending on experience, you may see certifications such as ECBA, CCBA or CBAP from IIBA, or other business-analysis / Agile / product certifications.

Do not treat certification as a substitute for real projects, communication and problem-solving.
Skills employers expect
ExcelSQLPower BIRequirementsProcess MappingCommunicationProblem Solving

Basic understanding of APIs, databases, Agile/Scrum and software development can help in IT Business Analyst roles.
You must clear
Class 10 → Class 12 in any suitable stream → BBA/B.Com/B.Tech/BCA/Economics/Statistics/related Bachelor's → learn Excel + SQL + Process Mapping + Requirements + Power BI → internship/project → Junior Business Analyst → Business Analyst.
Very importantDo not learn only tools. Knowing Excel, SQL and Power BI is useful, but a Business Analyst must first understand the business problem, ask the right questions and translate needs into a clear solution.
CIRCLE 3 · BUSINESS ANALYST JOBS

What kind of Business Analyst could you become?

Business Analysts work in almost every industry. The title changes depending on whether the focus is software, operations, products, finance or data.

IT Business Analyst
Work between business users and software/technology teams. Gather requirements, explain workflows, clarify features and support testing.
RequirementsSystemsUAT
Functional Analyst
Understand how a particular business function works — for example Finance, HR, Supply Chain or ERP — and help configure/improve systems that support it.
Process Analyst
Study how work moves through an organisation, find delays or unnecessary steps and redesign the process to make it faster, cheaper or more reliable.
Operations Analyst
Use data and process knowledge to improve everyday business operations such as delivery, staffing, inventory, customer service or production.
Product Analyst
Study how customers use a digital product, analyse product metrics and help product teams decide what to improve.
Data-oriented Business Analyst
Use SQL, Excel, Power BI/Tableau and business metrics to find patterns and support decisions. This role sits between traditional BA and Data Analyst work.
Banking / Financial Services BA
Work on banking, payments, lending, insurance, risk, compliance or financial systems. Domain knowledge becomes very important.
Healthcare Business Analyst
Improve hospital, insurance, health-tech or healthcare operations by translating clinical/business needs into processes, reports or systems.
Supply Chain Business Analyst
Analyse procurement, inventory, warehouses, logistics and delivery performance to improve cost and service.
Consulting Analyst
Work on business problems across clients, collect information, analyse options and help prepare recommendations or implementation plans.
Business Analyst vs Data Analyst
Business Analyst: focuses on business problems, requirements, processes and solutions.
Data Analyst: focuses more deeply on collecting, cleaning, analysing and visualising data.

Many modern roles overlap, especially when BAs use SQL and dashboards.
Business Analyst vs Product Manager
Business Analyst: usually clarifies requirements, processes and solution details.
Product Manager: owns broader product direction, priorities, customer value and roadmap.

Experienced BAs sometimes move into Product Management.
Business Analyst vs Project Manager
Business Analyst: asks “What problem are we solving and what should the solution do?”
Project Manager: asks “How do we deliver this work on time, within scope and with the right resources?”
Business Analyst vs Financial Analyst
Business Analyst: broader process, systems, requirements and business-improvement role.
Financial Analyst: focuses mainly on financial performance, budgets, forecasts and investment/business finance.
Where you can work
IT services, product companies, banks, fintech, insurance, healthcare, consulting, ecommerce, manufacturing, logistics, telecom, Global Capability Centres (GCCs), government technology projects and startups.
AI-era reality
AI can generate first-draft requirements, meeting summaries, process diagrams and reports. But it cannot fully replace the human work of resolving conflicting stakeholder needs, understanding organisational context, deciding what problem matters and checking whether a proposed solution actually solves it.

Future BAs who know AI tools + data + business processes will be stronger.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Business Analyst salaries vary by industry, city, technical skills, degree, domain knowledge and whether the role is in IT, consulting, banking or product companies.

Intern / trainee
₹10,000 – ₹25,000/month
A practical broad range for many internships and trainee roles. Premium companies may pay more; smaller firms may pay less.
Starting / Junior Business Analyst
₹3.5 – ₹7 lakh/year
A broad realistic range for entry-level roles. Strong SQL, internships, technical understanding or good campus placement can improve starting pay.
Experienced Business Analyst
₹7 – ₹15 lakh+/year
Analysts with strong domain knowledge, requirements expertise, SQL/data skills and project experience can move into higher-paying roles.
Senior BA / Lead / Product-facing roles
₹12 – ₹25 lakh+/year
Senior BAs, Lead BAs, Product Analysts and specialised consulting or banking-domain roles can earn more depending on employer and experience.
High-end roles
Can be substantially higher
Top product companies, consulting firms, fintech and global capability centres can offer higher compensation, but these roles are competitive and usually expect strong experience or specialist domain knowledge.
Course cost
You do not need an expensive private “Business Analyst course” to begin. A good bachelor's degree plus Excel, SQL, Power BI, requirements, process mapping, internships and real projects can be enough to enter the field.
Money warningBe careful with institutes promising guaranteed ₹10–15 lakh packages after a short BA course. Employers care about problem-solving, communication, domain knowledge, tools and project evidence — not only a certificate.

How to read your four circles

Business + problem-solving + communication — Business Analysis may be a strong fit.

Love numbers and dashboards more than stakeholder discussions — compare Data Analyst.

Love product decisions and customer strategy — compare Product Manager.

One extra rule: a good Business Analyst does not begin with “Which tool should we use?” — they begin with “What problem are we actually trying to solve?”

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, practise understanding everyday problems clearly before worrying about certifications.

1Right now, in school
  • Become comfortable with percentages, tables, graphs and spreadsheets
  • Practise explaining a process clearly — for example how a school library issues and returns books
  • When something goes wrong, ask “Why?” several times before jumping to a solution
2After Class 10
  • Choose MEC/Commerce if you enjoy business and numbers, or MPC if you want a stronger technology route
  • After Class 12, compare BBA, B.Com, B.Tech, BCA, Economics, Statistics and Computer Science
  • Choose a degree you would still value even if you later decide not to become a Business Analyst
3During college
  • Learn Excel, SQL, Power BI, process mapping and requirements writing
  • Build projects where you identify a business problem and propose a measurable solution
  • Get internships in operations, IT, consulting, banking, product or analytics teams
Best first testChoose one real school problem — for example long lunch queues, missing library books or late fee collection. Talk to the people involved, draw the current process, identify where the problem occurs and suggest one change that can be measured. If you enjoy understanding the problem before proposing the solution, Business Analysis may genuinely suit you.

This is career guidance, not an official admission or salary notice. There is no single compulsory qualification or certification for every Business Analyst role. Degree requirements vary by employer. Certifications such as ECBA/CCBA/CBAP can be useful later but are not substitutes for real project experience. Salary figures are broad practical India ranges and vary substantially by city, employer, industry, skills and experience.
