Indian Railways Jobs

Indian Railways is not one career and there is no single “Railway exam.” Recruitment happens for different job families — operations, stations, ticketing, clerical work, locomotive driving, technicians, engineering, stores, healthcare and other specialist roles. The right route depends mainly on whether you complete Class 10, ITI, Class 12, Diploma, Engineering or Graduation.

CIRCLE 1

Would Railway work suit you?

A railway job can offer stable government employment, but many posts involve shifts, strict rules, medical standards, public responsibility and transfers.

Can you follow safety rules exactly even when work becomes repetitive?

Would you be comfortable working early mornings, nights, weekends or rotating shifts if your post requires it?

Can you stay calm when passengers, trains, equipment or safety decisions create pressure?

Are you willing to prepare for competitive exams with Maths, reasoning, general awareness or technical subjects?

Can you accept that some railway posts have strict eyesight, colour-vision, hearing or other medical standards?

Would you be comfortable being posted away from your home town depending on vacancy and railway zone?

CIRCLE 2

Which route can you take?

Indian Railways does not have one single career path. First identify your qualification level, then target the correct railway recruitment and build toward the job you want.

Exam wordsCBT = Computer Based Test. It is the railway written examination taken on a computer, usually with multiple-choice questions. CBT-1 means the first-stage Computer Based Test and CBT-2 means the second-stage Computer Based Test. CBAT = Computer Based Aptitude Test, a separate aptitude test used for certain posts such as Assistant Loco Pilot and Station Master where prescribed.
After Class 10
Route A — Level-1 railway jobs

These are entry-level railway field, maintenance and support posts recruited through Railway Recruitment Boards / Railway Recruitment Cells when notified.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → check the current Level-1 notification → meet the exact post-wise qualification → Computer Based Test (CBT) → Physical Efficiency Test where prescribed → Document Verification → Medical Examination → appointment.

Good for: students who want an earlier-entry government railway route and are comfortable with field, maintenance or support work.
Class 10 + ITI / Technical route
Route B — Assistant Loco Pilot (ALP)

ALP is a technical train-running role. The exact eligible ITI trades / apprenticeship / diploma qualifications are specified in the current RRB notification.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → eligible ITI / Act Apprenticeship or relevant technical qualification → RRB ALP application → CBT-1 → CBT-2 → Computer Based Aptitude Test where applicable → Document Verification → strict railway medical examination → Assistant Loco Pilot.

Good for: students interested in locomotives, railway operations, technical systems and shift work.
Class 10 + ITI / Diploma
Route C — Railway Technician

Technician recruitment covers multiple technical categories. Qualification depends on the exact post and may include specified ITI trades, apprenticeship or diploma-level technical education.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → eligible ITI / technical diploma → RRB Technician notification → CBT → Document Verification → Medical Examination → Technician post.

Good for: students who prefer electrical, mechanical, electronics, signals, workshop or equipment-maintenance work.
After Class 12
Route D — NTPC Undergraduate posts

Current NTPC Undergraduate categories include posts such as Commercial cum Ticket Clerk, Accounts Clerk cum Typist, Junior Clerk cum Typist and Trains Clerk, subject to the current notification.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Class 12 in any eligible stream → RRB NTPC Undergraduate application → CBT-1 → CBT-2 → typing test only for posts that prescribe it → Document Verification → Medical Examination → allotted railway post.

Important: the typing test applies only to designated typist posts; it is not required for every NTPC Undergraduate post.
Diploma / Engineering
Route E — Junior Engineer (JE) / technical graduate-level posts

JE recruitment is for technical candidates in branches such as Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, Electronics and related engineering fields depending on the post.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Diploma or B.E./B.Tech in the required engineering branch → RRB JE notification → CBT-1 → CBT-2 → Document Verification → Medical Examination → Junior Engineer / other allotted technical post.

Good for: students who want railway engineering, construction, maintenance, rolling stock, signalling, electrical or infrastructure work.
After Graduation
Route F — NTPC Graduate posts

Graduate NTPC recruitment can include roles such as Station Master, Goods Train Manager, Chief Commercial cum Ticket Supervisor, Junior Account Assistant cum Typist and Senior Clerk cum Typist, depending on the current notification.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Class 12 → recognised bachelor's degree → RRB NTPC Graduate application → CBT-1 → CBT-2 → aptitude test / typing test only for the posts that prescribe it → Document Verification → Medical Examination → allotted graduate railway post.
Station Master route
Route G — Station Master

Station Master is an operations role responsible for safe and efficient train movement and station working. It is normally recruited through the graduate NTPC family when notified.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Class 12 → bachelor's degree in any eligible discipline → RRB NTPC Graduate → CBT-1 → CBT-2 → Computer Based Aptitude Test for Station Master → Document Verification → prescribed railway medical examination → Station Master.

Good for: students who can handle responsibility, shift work, rules, communication and operational pressure.
Graduate Operations route
Route H — Section Controller

Section Controller is an operations-control career focused on regulating train movements over a railway section and coordinating safe, efficient traffic flow.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Class 12 → bachelor's degree → apply when RRB Section Controller recruitment is notified → prescribed CBT / aptitude stages → Document Verification → medical standards → Section Controller.

Good for: students who like real-time coordination, decision-making, rules and high-responsibility operations work.
Healthcare route
Route I — Railway Paramedical jobs

Railways recruit healthcare professionals separately from NTPC. Posts can include Nursing Superintendent, Pharmacist, Laboratory roles, Dialysis Technician, Radiographer and other paramedical categories, depending on the notification.

Roadmap:
Class 10 → Class 12 with the required science subjects → complete the professional healthcare qualification required for your chosen post → obtain registration where required → RRB Paramedical recruitment → CBT → Document Verification → Medical Examination → railway healthcare post.
Security route
Route J — Railway Protection Force (RPF)

RPF recruitment is separate from normal NTPC and technical railway recruitment. The two common career levels are Constable and Sub-Inspector when notified.

Roadmap — RPF Constable:
Class 10 → meet current educational/age eligibility → CBT → Physical Measurement Test + Physical Efficiency Test → Document Verification → Medical Examination → RPF Constable.

Roadmap — RPF Sub-Inspector:
Class 10 → Class 12 → bachelor's degree → RPF SI recruitment → CBT → Physical Measurement Test + Physical Efficiency Test → Document Verification → Medical Examination → RPF Sub-Inspector.
Simple decision rule
If you stop at Class 10: look at Level-1 / eligible ITI pathways.

If you complete ITI: compare ALP and Technician routes.

If you complete Class 12: compare NTPC Undergraduate and eligible Level-1/technical routes.

If you complete Diploma / Engineering: compare JE, ALP and technical railway roles.

If you complete any bachelor's degree: compare NTPC Graduate, Station Master, Section Controller and RPF SI.

If you study Nursing / Pharmacy / allied health: track Railway Paramedical recruitment.
Very importantMedical standards are different for different railway posts. ALP and certain operations/safety categories have stricter eyesight and medical requirements than many clerical posts. Check the medical category of your target post early — not after years of preparation.
CIRCLE 3 · RAILWAY JOB FAMILIES

What kind of Railway work could you do?

Indian Railways employs people in operations, engineering, customer service, accounts, healthcare and field maintenance. The work experience is very different across these families.

Station Master
Station operations and safe train movement. Coordinate train reception/dispatch, operational communication and station responsibilities. Shift work and strong safety responsibility are normal.
Section Controller
Works in railway control, monitoring and coordinating train movements over a section. This is an operations-control job with high concentration and safety responsibility.
Assistant Loco Pilot
Works in locomotive/train operations and assists the driving function. It is a technical, safety-critical role with A1 medical standards, irregular hours and high alertness requirements.
Goods Train Manager
Works in train operations related to goods/freight services and operational responsibility for assigned trains. Shift, travel and safety procedures are important.
Commercial / Ticketing
Commercial cum Ticket Clerk and related commercial posts deal with passengers, ticketing, booking, revenue and customer-facing railway work.
Clerical / Accounts
Accounts Clerk, Junior Clerk, Senior Clerk and Junior Accounts Assistant-type roles focus on records, bills, data, correspondence and office systems. Some require computer typing proficiency.
Junior Engineer
Technical supervision and maintenance in disciplines such as Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, Electronics / Signal & Telecom, depending on the post. Diploma/engineering knowledge is central.
Technician
Hands-on maintenance and troubleshooting of railway electrical, mechanical, electronics, signalling, workshop or equipment systems. ITI/apprenticeship skills are especially relevant.
Depot Material Superintendent
Works with engineering stores/material management, inventory and supply systems. Current JE/DMS recruitment accepts prescribed engineering Diploma qualifications.
Chemical / Metallurgical Assistant
Laboratory/material-testing role supporting railway quality and materials work. Current recruitment maps this category to specified Chemistry/Physics qualifications.
Level-1 Field Jobs
Various maintenance/support categories can involve track, workshop, electrical, mechanical, signalling or operational-support work. These can be physically demanding and may require PET plus post-specific medical standards.
Paramedical
Railways also operate a large healthcare system. Professional routes include nursing, dialysis, public-health/inspection and other paramedical categories when notified.
Station Master vs ALP
Station Master: operations, coordination, station responsibility; graduate NTPC route.
ALP: locomotive/technical operating role; ITI/Diploma/Engineering route; stricter A1 medical standard.
Neither is simply “better” — they are very different jobs.
JE vs Technician
Junior Engineer: more technical supervision, engineering responsibility and higher entry pay; usually Diploma/Degree-level route.
Technician: more hands-on maintenance/trade work; often ITI/apprenticeship route.
Ticket Clerk vs Accounts/Junior Clerk
Commercial cum Ticket Clerk: more passenger/commercial facing.
Accounts/Junior Clerk: more office/records-oriented and current NTPC UG requires typing skill test for those typist posts.
Shift reality
Railways operate 24×7. Operations, stations, locomotives, control, maintenance and many field posts can involve nights, weekends and public holidays. Clerical/office posts may have more regular schedules depending on office.
Transfer reality
Posting depends on the RRB, railway zone, production unit, post preference and vacancy. Students should not assume they will always get their home district or preferred city.
Competition reality
Railway recruitment attracts very large numbers of applicants. A minimum qualification only makes you eligible; selection depends on competitive performance, skill/aptitude/PET where applicable, document verification and medical fitness.
Your Plan B
Choose education that also works outside Railways:
ITI TradeDiploma EngineeringB.TechAccountingComputer SkillsHealthcare
Golden ruleDo not study “for Railways” first and decide the qualification later. Build a useful qualification first, then target the railway job family that matches it.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Railway pay is based on the 7th CPC pay level for the specific post. “Initial basic pay” is not the final take-home salary; applicable allowances and deductions are added separately.

Level-1 posts
₹18,000 basic/month
Current CEN 09/2025 uses Pay Level 1 for various entry-level railway posts.
ALP / Level-2 examples
₹19,900 basic/month
Current ALP CEN 01/2026 is Level 2. Accounts Clerk, Junior Clerk and Trains Clerk in current NTPC Undergraduate recruitment are also Level 2.
Commercial cum Ticket Clerk
₹21,700 basic/month
Current NTPC Undergraduate CEN 07/2025: Pay Level 3.
Graduate NTPC — Level 5 examples
₹29,200 basic/month
Recent graduate NTPC categories such as Goods Train Manager, Junior Accounts Assistant cum Typist and Senior Clerk cum Typist have used Pay Level 5.
Station Master / Chief Commercial / JE / Section Controller
₹35,400 basic/month
Recent Station Master / Chief Commercial graduate NTPC posts, current JE/DMS/CMA and current Section Controller recruitment use Pay Level 6.
Allowances
Depending on rules/posting, railway employees may receive applicable DA, HRA, transport or railway-specific allowances and other service benefits. Actual take-home varies by posting and deductions.
Preparation cost
You do not need expensive “Railway coaching” to become eligible. The essential investment is the correct school/ITI/Diploma/Degree qualification plus exam preparation. Use official RRB syllabus and previous papers before paying large coaching fees.
Money warningNever pay an agent, broker or tout for a Railway job. RRB recruitment notices repeatedly warn that recruitment is computerised and selection is based on the official process and merit.

How to choose your Railway route

Want hands-on technical work after Class 10? — compare ITI → Technician / ALP.

Want passenger/office work after Class 12? — compare NTPC Undergraduate.

Like engineering? — Diploma/B.Tech → JE / DMS / technical roles.

Want train/station operations after graduation? — compare Station Master, Goods Train Manager and Section Controller-type routes.

One extra rule: there is no single “best Railway job.” The right job depends on qualification, medical fitness, shift tolerance and whether you prefer field, technical, operations or office work.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, the smartest first decision is choosing the right education route — not starting full-time competitive-exam coaching.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen basic Maths, reasoning, English/Hindi and general awareness
  • Learn what technical, operations, clerical and engineering railway jobs actually do
  • If interested in ALP/operations, get an early eye check — especially colour vision — instead of discovering a medical issue years later
2After Class 10
  • Skill-first: choose a useful ITI trade if you want Technician/ALP-type technical work
  • 12th route: continue Intermediate in a stream that also supports a backup career, then target NTPC UG
  • Engineering route: choose Polytechnic Diploma or MPC → Engineering if JE/technical officer work interests you
3When eligible
  • Track only official RRB/RRC websites and read the full CEN for your post
  • Check qualification + age + medical standard + exam stages before applying
  • Practise typing only if your target post requires it; practise PET only if your recruitment includes it
Best first testPick three Railway jobs that sound interesting — for example ALP, Station Master and Junior Engineer. For each one, write down: minimum qualification, daily work, medical standard, exam stages and initial pay. If one route still interests you after seeing the real work rather than just “government job,” that is the route worth exploring further.

This is career guidance, not an official recruitment notice. Railway qualifications, vacancies, age limits, medical standards, exam stages and post names can change between Centralised Employment Notices (CENs). Current examples in this report use RRB notices available in 2025–26, including CEN 09/2025 Level-1, CEN 07/2025 NTPC Undergraduate, CEN 06/2025 NTPC Graduate, CEN 05/2025 JE/DMS/CMA, CEN 04/2025 Section Controller, CEN 03/2025 Paramedical, CEN 02/2025 Technician and CEN 01/2026 Assistant Loco Pilot. Always verify the latest detailed CEN on an official Railway Recruitment Board website.
