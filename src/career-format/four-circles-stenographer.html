Stenographer

Stenographers listen to spoken words at high speed, write them in shorthand and then accurately transcribe them on a computer. They work in government departments, courts, tribunals, legislative offices and senior administrative offices. The career rewards speed, concentration, language accuracy, confidentiality and computer skills.

CIRCLE 2

Skills & pathway

Stenography is unusual because a short qualification can be enough for some central-government posts, but the shorthand skill test is essential.

1Shorthand
A system of symbols/short forms used to capture speech quickly. Competitive stenography exams measure your dictation speed and transcription accuracy.
2Typing
After taking dictation in shorthand, you must transcribe it accurately on a computer. Good typing speed, spelling and formatting are important.
3English / Hindi
SSC tests stenography in English or Hindi. Strong grammar and comprehension also help in the written exam and daily office work.
After Class 10
Continue Intermediate / Class 12 in any recognised stream. CEC/Humanities can be comfortable for students who like office/government work, but MPC or BiPC students are also fully eligible for the main SSC stenographer route.

You can begin learning English shorthand and computer typing alongside Class 11–12.
Main national route
SSC Stenographer Grade C & D Examination.

The 2026 SSC notification requires candidates to have passed 12th standard or equivalent. SSC recruits Grade C and Grade D stenographers for various Central Government Ministries, Departments and Organisations.
SSC written exam
The 2026 Computer Based Examination has:
General Intelligence & Reasoning — 50 questions
General Awareness — 50 questions
English Language & Comprehension — 100 questions

There is negative marking under the current notification.
SSC shorthand skill test
Grade C: 10-minute dictation at 100 words per minute.
Grade D: 10-minute dictation at 80 words per minute.

The dictated passage is then transcribed on a computer. Under the 2026 SSC notification, the skill test is mandatory but qualifying in nature; final merit is based on the Computer Based Examination after qualifying the skill test.
Current SSC age example
For the 2026 SSC examination, the basic age limits are 18–30 years for Grade C and 18–27 years for Grade D, with applicable category relaxations under the notification. These limits can change, so always check the exam year.
Andhra Pradesh court route
AP High Court / District Judiciary Stenographer Grade-III is a separate recruitment route.

The 2025 A.P. District Judiciary recruitment carried a pay scale of ₹34,580–₹1,07,210 and completed its stenography skill-test/selection process during 2026. Court recruitments may require graduation plus prescribed shorthand/typing qualifications, so do not assume SSC's Class 12 eligibility applies to court posts.
Technical training
Students commonly train for English shorthand, typewriting and computer applications through recognised technical/vocational institutes or coaching centres. The value of the course comes from reaching the required speed and accuracy, not just obtaining a certificate.
You must clear
SSC route: Class 10 → Class 12 + shorthand practice → SSC Stenographer C/D Computer Based Exam → stenography skill test → document verification/allocation → Stenographer.

AP Court route: Class 12 → degree where required + prescribed English shorthand/typewriting qualification → court recruitment examination/skill test → Stenographer Grade-III.
ImportantDo not wait until the notification arrives to learn shorthand. Reaching 80–100 w.p.m. with low error rates usually requires months of regular practice.
CIRCLE 3 · JOB AREAS

Where can a Stenographer work?

Stenographers are most strongly associated with government and legal offices, but the underlying skills also support executive and administrative careers.

SSC Stenographer Grade D
Entry central-government stenography route after Class 12. Work can include dictation, transcription, correspondence, files, meeting notes and assistance to officers in ministries/departments.
Central Govt.80 w.p.m.Group C
SSC Stenographer Grade C
Higher stenography grade under SSC recruitment, classified in the current 2026 notice as a Group B, Non-Gazetted post. The skill-test speed is 100 w.p.m.
Court Stenographer
Work with judges and court administration, prepare dictated material and handle sensitive legal documentation. Accuracy, confidentiality and legal terminology become especially important.
Personal Assistant / PA
Experienced stenographers may progress into Personal Assistant roles supporting senior officers with correspondence, scheduling, meetings and confidential documents.
Private Secretary / PS
Senior secretarial role supporting high-level officers, executives or government officials. Requires excellent office management, confidentiality and communication in addition to stenography.
Court Master
Some High Courts recruit specialised Court Master / Personal Secretary-type roles with higher qualification and stenography requirements. These are separate recruitments, not automatic promotions from every stenographer post.
Legislative / Parliamentary Work
Stenography and reporting skills may be used in legislative secretariats, assemblies, parliamentary offices and similar institutions when vacancies are notified.
Legal / Corporate Stenography
Law firms, tribunals, senior advocates and some private organisations may employ stenographers, transcription specialists or executive assistants, although private demand varies.
Stenographer vs Typist
Typist: types written or supplied material quickly and accurately.
Stenographer: can first capture spoken dictation in shorthand at high speed and then transcribe it.
Stenography is a more specialised skill.
Stenographer vs Secretary
Stenographer: specialised in shorthand, transcription and official documentation.
Secretary / Executive Assistant: may focus more broadly on schedules, meetings, communication and office coordination.
Senior stenographers often develop both skill sets.
Where SSC recruits
The current SSC Stenographer examination fills posts across Central Government Ministries, Departments, attached/subordinate offices and statutory bodies located across India.
AI-era reality
Speech-to-text and AI transcription will reduce some routine transcription work. However, government/court roles still require accuracy, confidentiality, official responsibility and human checking. Future-ready stenographers should add fast computer work, document formatting, digital filing, transcription software, office tools and strong language skills rather than depending on shorthand alone.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Government stenographer salaries are determined by the specific department, grade and pay level. The same title can carry different scales in different services.

Central Grade D — common example
Pay Level 4 · ₹25,500 basic at entry
Many Central Government Stenographer Grade D posts use Level 4: ₹25,500–₹81,100, plus applicable allowances. Always check the department allocated through the SSC examination because pay structure can vary by cadre.
Central Grade C
Often Level 6 / Level 7 depending on cadre
Central-government rules are not identical across every department. Official examples show Grade C stenographer cadres at Level 6 and some at Level 7. Use the actual post/department notification for the final pay level.
AP District Court Steno Grade-III
₹34,580 – ₹1,07,210 pay scale
The Andhra Pradesh High Court's 2025 direct recruitment notification for District Judiciary Stenographer Grade-III listed this official scale.
Allowances
Government posts may include applicable DA, HRA, transport and other allowances according to the service and posting. Basic pay is not the same as final take-home salary.
Training cost
Stenography can be one of the lower-cost career-preparation routes compared with a professional degree. Costs mainly involve shorthand/typewriting training, books, computer practice and exam preparation. Expensive coaching is not compulsory.
Money warningDo not pay a large amount to an institute that promises a guaranteed government stenographer job. Training can teach the skill; selection still depends on the official competitive examination and skill test.

How to read your four circles

Language + speed + accuracy — Stenography may suit you.

Like office work but dislike shorthand practice — compare Junior Assistant / Clerk / Bank Clerk / Office Administration.

Love legal environments — Court Stenographer can be a particularly relevant route.

One extra rule: stenography is a performance skill — you become employable by reaching the required speed accurately, not by memorising theory alone.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, there is no need to begin competitive-exam coaching yet. First test whether you enjoy language, typing and speed practice.

1Right now, in school
  • Improve English grammar, spelling and listening
  • Learn proper computer keyboard typing rather than two-finger typing
  • Practise listening to one-minute audio and writing the key information accurately
2After Class 10
  • Continue Class 11–12 in any stream
  • Start a recognised or well-taught English shorthand + typewriting programme
  • Gradually target 80 w.p.m., then 100 w.p.m., while keeping error rates low
3After Class 12
  • For central jobs, track SSC Stenographer Grade C & D notifications
  • Consider completing a bachelor's degree alongside preparation because it opens court, PA and higher administrative routes
  • Track Andhra Pradesh High Court / District Judiciary stenographer notifications separately because their qualifications differ from SSC
Best first testTake a 5-minute English speech. Try to capture as much as possible while listening, then type a clean version afterward. Next, learn basic shorthand and repeat the same exercise for a month. If improving your speed + accuracy feels satisfying, Stenography may genuinely suit you.
