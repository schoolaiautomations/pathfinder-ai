Data Scientist

Data Scientists use mathematics, statistics, programming and domain knowledge to find useful patterns in data, make predictions and help organisations make better decisions. In the AI era, the role is moving beyond dashboards into machine learning, experimentation, forecasting, recommendation systems and AI-powered products.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want Data Science?

Data Science is not just “using AI.” A lot of the work is cleaning messy data, checking assumptions, writing code and explaining what the numbers actually mean.

Do you enjoy Maths, especially probability, averages, graphs and patterns?

Would you enjoy learning programming and debugging code when it gives the wrong result?

Do you like asking “What does the data actually prove?” instead of accepting the first explanation?

Can you patiently clean and organise imperfect data before doing the interesting analysis?

Would you enjoy explaining a technical result in simple words to a non-technical person?

Are you comfortable being wrong sometimes and testing whether a model really works on new data?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

There is no single compulsory “Data Scientist degree.” Strong Data Scientists come from Computer Science, Statistics, Mathematics, Engineering and Data Science programmes.

1Mathematics
Probability, statistics, algebra, calculus and matrices are the foundation for understanding data and machine-learning models.
2Programming
Python is the most common first language. SQL is essential for working with databases. Later you may use libraries for statistics, machine learning and data visualisation.
3Communication
A Data Scientist must explain what the model found, how reliable it is and what action the organisation should take.
After Class 10
Choose MPC — Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry — if Data Science is a serious goal. Maths keeps the widest options open for B.Tech CSE/AI/Data Science and B.Sc. Statistics/Mathematics/Data Science.
Engineering route
MPC → AP EAPCET / JEE / college admission → B.Tech/B.E. in CSE, Data Science, AI & ML, IT, Mathematics & Computing or related branch → projects/internship → Data Analyst / Junior Data Scientist / ML role.
Science route
Class 12 with Mathematics → B.Sc. Statistics / Mathematics / Computer Science / Data Science → M.Sc. or job depending skill level.

This route can be especially strong for students who enjoy statistics and modelling more than software engineering.
Andhra Pradesh entrance
AP EAPCET is the main state engineering entrance route for participating Andhra Pradesh engineering colleges. Many colleges now offer CSE specialisations such as AI, Machine Learning and Data Science, but branch names and seat matrices change every year.
National route
JEE Main / JEE Advanced are relevant for NITs, IIITs, IITs and other participating engineering institutes. For B.Sc./B.S. routes, some universities use CUET-UG while others use merit or their own admission process.
Higher study
Useful advanced routes include M.Sc. Data Science, Statistics, Mathematics, Computer Science, M.Tech AI/Data Science or specialised master's programmes. A Master's can help for advanced modelling and research roles, but it is not compulsory for every Data Scientist job.
Core skills to build
PythonSQLStatisticsExcelMachine LearningData VisualisationGit
You must clear
Class 10 → MPC → B.Tech/B.Sc. in a strong quantitative field → Python + SQL + Statistics + Machine Learning projects → internship / entry-level data role → Data Scientist.
ImportantDo not choose a weak college only because the branch name says “Data Science & AI.” Strong Maths, programming, projects and internships matter more than a fashionable course title.
CIRCLE 3 · DATA CAREERS

What kind of data professional could you become?

“Data Science” is a family of careers. These roles overlap, but they are not the same.

Data Analyst
Study existing data and explain what happened. Common tools: Excel, SQL, dashboards, statistics and business reporting.
SQLExcelDashboards
Data Scientist
Use statistics and machine learning to explain, predict or optimise outcomes. Work can include forecasting, experimentation, classification, recommendation and decision models.
Machine Learning Engineer
Turn ML models into reliable software systems used by real customers. Usually more software-engineering-heavy than a typical Data Scientist role.
Data Engineer
Build pipelines, databases and platforms that collect and prepare data for analysts, scientists and AI systems. Strong SQL, cloud and software skills are important.
Business Intelligence Analyst
Build dashboards and decision-support reporting for finance, sales, operations, supply chain and management teams.
Product Data Scientist
Analyse how people use apps/products, run experiments and help product teams decide what to build or change.
Marketing / Customer Data Scientist
Work on customer segmentation, churn prediction, campaign targeting, pricing and recommendation systems.
Risk / Fraud Data Scientist
Use data and ML to detect suspicious transactions, credit risk and fraud in banking, fintech and insurance.
Operations / Supply Chain Data Scientist
Forecast demand, optimise inventory, routes, staffing, factory schedules and logistics.
Healthcare Data Scientist
Analyse medical, hospital or public-health data. Some roles work on risk prediction, imaging, clinical operations or drug research and may require additional healthcare knowledge.
AI / Applied Scientist
Work on advanced machine learning, language models, recommendation systems, computer vision or generative AI. Stronger Maths and research skills are often expected.
Research Data Scientist
Develop new statistical/ML methods, publish research and work on difficult scientific or industrial problems. Master's/PhD-level training is common in advanced research roles.
Data Scientist vs Data Analyst
Data Analyst: usually focuses more on reporting, dashboards and explaining past/current performance.
Data Scientist: usually adds deeper statistics, experiments and predictive modelling.
In real companies, the titles can overlap.
Data Scientist vs ML Engineer
Data Scientist: focuses on finding patterns, building models and answering business/scientific questions.
ML Engineer: focuses more on deploying, scaling and maintaining machine-learning systems in production.
Where you can work
Technology, banking, fintech, ecommerce, healthcare, manufacturing, consulting, telecom, retail, logistics, insurance, media, government analytics, sports and research.
AI-era reality
AI will automate some basic analysis and coding, but it also increases the amount of data and AI systems that need to be tested, measured and improved. Strong Data Scientists will spend less time on repetitive work and more time on problem definition, experimentation, model evaluation and decision-making.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Data Science can pay well, but salary depends heavily on skill level, company, city, education and whether the role is really Data Science or a lower-level analytics job.

Intern / trainee
₹10,000 – ₹30,000/month
Internships vary widely. Strong companies may pay more, while academic or small-company internships may pay less.
Starting Data / Analytics role
₹4 – ₹8 lakh/year
A realistic broad range for many fresh graduates entering analytics, junior data science or related roles. Strong coding, statistics and internships can move this higher.
India Data Scientist benchmark
≈ ₹12.34 lakh/year average
Indeed's September 2026 India-wide Data Scientist benchmark is about ₹12,33,675/year, based on 374 reported salaries. This includes experienced professionals, so a fresher should not assume ₹12 lakh as a starting salary.
Experienced Data Scientist
₹8 – ₹20 lakh+/year
Good Data Scientists with several years of experience, strong ML/statistics and business impact can move well above entry-level pay.
Senior / specialist
₹15 – ₹35 lakh+/year
Senior Data Scientists, Applied Scientists, ML specialists and strong product-company roles can go substantially higher. These jobs are highly selective.
Course cost
You do not need an expensive private “Data Science bootcamp” to enter the field. A strong B.Tech/B.Sc. foundation plus Python, SQL, statistics and projects can be enough to start. Compare degree quality, placements and total fees before borrowing heavily.
Money warningDo not believe ads promising “₹20 lakh Data Scientist job in 6 months.” Entry-level competition is strong, and many beginners first enter through Data Analyst, BI or software roles before moving into Data Science.

How to read your four circles

Maths + coding + curiosity — Data Science may be a strong fit.

Love dashboards and business reporting — compare Data Analyst / Business Intelligence.

Love coding more than statistics — compare Software Engineering / ML Engineering / Data Engineering.

One extra rule: a good Data Scientist does not ask only “Can I build a model?” — they ask “Is this the right problem, and can I trust the answer?”

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

A school student can test Data Science without paying for an expensive course.

1Right now, in school
  • Become strong in Maths, graphs, percentages, averages and probability
  • Start basic Python
  • Use Excel or Google Sheets to analyse a small dataset and create charts
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC
  • Compare B.Tech CSE / AI / Data Science with B.Sc. Statistics / Mathematics / Data Science
  • Prepare for AP EAPCET / JEE / CUET or university admission based on the programme you target
3During college
  • Learn Python, SQL, statistics, machine learning and data visualisation
  • Build 4–6 projects using real datasets — not only tutorial copies
  • Get internships in analytics, product, finance, healthcare, manufacturing or another domain that interests you
Best first testTake a simple dataset — for example your class marks, cricket scores, weather data or household electricity use. Clean it, make three graphs and answer one question with evidence. If you enjoy turning messy numbers into a clear conclusion, Data Science may genuinely suit you.
