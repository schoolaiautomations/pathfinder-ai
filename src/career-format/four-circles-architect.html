Architect

Architects design buildings and spaces that must be useful, safe, comfortable and beautiful. The job combines creativity with Maths, technical drawings, building rules, materials, client needs, site coordination and endless revisions. If you only enjoy drawing but dislike technical problem-solving, architecture may feel very different from what you expect.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want architecture?

Many students imagine sketching beautiful buildings. Real architecture also means measurements, software, regulations, client changes and construction-site problems. Tap your honest answer.

Do you notice buildings, rooms, streets and public spaces and wonder why they were designed that way?

Do you enjoy both creative design and technical problem-solving rather than only one of them?

Can you patiently improve the same design many times after criticism from teachers, clients or engineers?

Are you comfortable with Maths, dimensions, scale, geometry and accurate drawings?

Would you enjoy learning CAD/BIM/3D software, building materials, structures, climate design and construction methods?

Can you accept a 5-year professional degree with studio work, models, presentations, site visits and deadlines that can sometimes be intense?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

Architecture has a specific academic route. Unlike many design careers, Class 11–12 subject choices can directly affect your B.Arch eligibility.

1Mathematics
Scale, proportion, geometry, areas, dimensions, structures and spatial thinking are part of architecture. Mathematics is also part of the eligibility and entrance-test pathway.
2Physics
Buildings must stand, respond to climate, light, heat, sound and forces. Physics helps you understand how design decisions behave in the real world.
3Drawing & Visual Thinking
You do not need to be a professional artist, but you should learn perspective, proportion, observation, composition and how to communicate an idea visually.
After Class 10
Intermediate MPC is the simplest and safest route for B.Arch. Under the current Council of Architecture eligibility, Class 12 students need Physics and Mathematics as compulsory subjects plus one approved third subject, with the required aggregate marks. After Class 12, clear the appropriate architecture aptitude/admission route and join a 5-year B.Arch degree.
Entrance exams
NATA — the Council of Architecture's aptitude test used for admission to B.Arch programmes at participating institutions.
JEE Main Paper 2A (B.Arch) — used for B.Arch admission at NITs, SPAs and other participating institutions.
You do not necessarily need both for every college. First shortlist colleges and check which score their admission process accepts.
B.Arch eligibility
For the current 2026 CoA pathway, a candidate must pass 10+2 with Physics and Mathematics compulsory plus one of the permitted additional subjects, with at least 45% aggregate; or pass a 10+3 Diploma with Mathematics compulsory and at least 45% aggregate. Admission must also satisfy the required architecture aptitude-test condition.
Diploma route
A 10+3 Diploma with Mathematics can also meet current B.Arch eligibility if the required aggregate and aptitude-test conditions are satisfied. However, B.Arch is a professional 5-year course and there is no normal lateral entry into the middle of the 5-year B.Arch degree.
You must clear
Class 10 → Class 12 with required Physics + Mathematics subjects → NATA / JEE Main Paper 2A or accepted architecture admission route → 5-year B.Arch from a recognised institution → Council of Architecture registration → Architect.
Check firstDo not join a course simply because it contains the words “architecture” or “architectural design.” If your goal is to become a registered Architect, verify that the qualification and institution are recognised by the Council of Architecture.
CIRCLE 3 · THE WORK

What do architects actually do?

Architecture is much broader than drawing house plans. Your work can move from a small residence to hospitals, offices, campuses, public spaces or entire urban districts.

Building Design
The classic architecture role. Design houses, apartments, offices, schools, hospitals, hotels, commercial buildings and institutions.
Work: concept design, plans, sections, elevations, materials, client meetings, approvals and site coordination.
Residential
Homes, villas and apartments. You work closely with clients on space planning, lifestyle, climate, materials, budgets and details.
Commercial / Institutional
Offices, retail, hotels, campuses, hospitals, schools and public buildings. These projects often involve larger consultant teams and more complex coordination.
Urban Design & Planning
Work at a larger scale — streets, neighbourhoods, public spaces and city development. Urban Planning may require a separate specialised planning qualification depending on the role.
Landscape Architecture
Design outdoor environments such as parks, campuses, waterfronts, gardens and public spaces. Architects may specialise through postgraduate study or professional experience.
Interior Architecture
Focus on how interior spaces are organised, detailed and experienced. This overlaps with interior design but can include stronger architectural, technical and construction coordination.
Sustainable Design
Energy-efficient buildings, daylight, ventilation, climate-responsive design, water use, materials and green-building performance.
Conservation
Work with heritage buildings, historic structures and adaptive reuse — documenting, restoring and sensitively redesigning older buildings.
BIM / Digital Architecture
Use tools such as Revit/BIM, parametric modelling and digital coordination to develop and manage complex building information.
AutoCADRevit/BIMSketchUpRhinoRendering
Project / Site Coordination
Coordinate architects, Civil/Structural engineers, MEP engineers, contractors, clients and vendors so the design can actually be constructed correctly.
Architect vs Civil Engineer
Architect: focuses heavily on space, function, user experience, form and overall building design.
Civil/Structural Engineer: focuses heavily on structure, loads, foundations, infrastructure and technical engineering performance.
They work together on buildings.
Architect vs Interior Designer
Architects train for whole buildings and the built environment, including professional architectural practice. Interior designers focus mainly on interior spaces, finishes, furniture and experience. Their education and professional pathways are different.
Registration
After a recognised architectural qualification, register with the Council of Architecture. CoA states that valid registration entitles a person to practise the profession of architecture and use the title/style Architect, subject to current registration requirements.
Who hires
Architecture studios, construction companies, real-estate developers, design consultancies, infrastructure/EPC companies, BIM firms, corporate design teams and government/public organisations. Experienced registered architects may also establish their own practice.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Architecture can become a strong long-term profession, but early-career salaries are often lower than students expect relative to the length and workload of the degree.

Architecture internship
₹5,000 – ₹15,000/month
Current internship listings vary widely. Indeed's available India data reports roughly ₹14,000/month average, but some internships are much lower.
Junior Architect
₹15,000 – ₹30,000/month
Indeed's 2026 India-wide average for Junior Architect is about ₹19,500/month. Strong portfolios, BIM skills and larger firms can pay more.
Architect — experienced
₹25,000 – ₹60,000+/month
Indeed currently reports an India-wide Architect average around ₹25,000/month, but actual pay varies heavily by city, firm, project type and experience.
Senior / specialist
₹40,000 – ₹1,00,000+/month
Senior Architects, BIM leads, project architects, design managers and specialists can move higher. Indeed's reported Senior Architect average is around ₹39,000/month, while some roles pay substantially more.
Own practice
Experienced registered architects can eventually build independent practices. Income can be much higher than employment — or very irregular — because it depends on clients, reputation, project pipeline, fees, business skills and collections.
The cost before that
B.Arch is a 5-year degree, one year longer than most B.Tech programmes. Besides college tuition, students should budget for a laptop/software, printing, model-making materials, stationery, site visits and presentation costs. These can add up over five years.
Ask at homeCan we afford the full 5-year cost if the first salary may be around ₹15,000–30,000/month? Compare total college fees, CoA recognition, studio quality, internship opportunities, software training and placement outcomes before choosing an expensive architecture school.

How to read your four circles

Creative + technical + patient — Architecture may be a strong fit.

Love drawing but dislike Maths/technical work — explore Graphic Design, Animation, Fashion Design or other creative careers too.

Love buildings but prefer calculations/structures — Civil or Structural Engineering may fit better.

One extra rule: Architecture is not “art instead of engineering.” It sits between design, technology, construction and people.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

You can test your fit for architecture before paying for coaching or choosing a college.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen Maths and Physics, especially geometry, scale, proportion and basic mechanics
  • Sketch real objects and spaces — your classroom, house, street or bus stop — rather than copying only pictures from the internet
  • Study one building each month and ask: Who uses it? Where does sunlight enter? How do people move through it? What would you improve?
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC if you want the simplest route that satisfies the current B.Arch subject pathway
  • Build drawing, observation, spatial reasoning, geometry and general architectural awareness for NATA / JEE Main Paper 2A
  • Learn one beginner design tool such as SketchUp or basic CAD, but do not neglect hand drawing and design thinking
3Before choosing a B.Arch college
  • Check that the programme/institution is recognised by the Council of Architecture
  • Compare studios, faculty, workshops, student portfolios, internships, site exposure and five-year total cost
  • Look at final-year student work from at least three colleges — it tells you more about the design culture than advertisements do
Best first testRedesign your own bedroom or classroom on paper. Measure it, draw a simple plan to scale, decide where furniture, windows and circulation should go, and explain why your new layout is better. If you enjoy both the creativity and the constraints, Architecture may suit you.
