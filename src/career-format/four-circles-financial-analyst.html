Financial Analyst

A Financial Analyst studies money, business performance and financial data to help a company or investor make better decisions. The work may include analysing revenue and costs, building budgets and forecasts, reading financial statements, valuing companies, preparing management reports or studying investments. It is a broad finance career — Corporate Financial Analyst, FP&A Analyst, Equity Research Analyst and Investment Analyst are related but different roles.

Would Financial Analysis suit you?

This career is not just “working in the stock market.” Many Financial Analysts work inside companies, studying budgets, costs, sales, profits and future plans.

Do you enjoy working with numbers, percentages, tables and graphs?

Would you enjoy understanding why a company made more or less profit this year?

Can you patiently work with spreadsheets and check calculations for mistakes?

Are you interested in business, economics, companies, markets or how money moves?

Would you be comfortable explaining a financial result clearly to a manager or client?

Are you willing to keep learning Excel, accounting, finance and data tools even after college?

CIRCLE 2

Which pathway can you take?

There is no single compulsory Financial Analyst degree or national entrance exam. Employers usually look for a relevant degree plus strong accounting, Excel, financial-analysis and communication skills.

Useful termsFP&A = Financial Planning & Analysis. CFA = Chartered Financial Analyst, a global professional credential from CFA Institute. NISM = National Institute of Securities Markets. These are optional or role-specific additions — they are not compulsory for every Financial Analyst job.
After Class 10
MEC / Commerce with Mathematics is the strongest direct route.

It builds Mathematics, Economics, Commerce and Accounts foundations useful for finance.

CEC / Commerce without Maths is also possible, but students should strengthen percentages, statistics and spreadsheet skills themselves.

MPC students can also enter Finance later through BBA, Economics, engineering + finance roles or MBA.
Route A — B.Com
Class 10 → MEC/CEC/Commerce → B.Com / B.Com Finance / Accounting → Excel + financial statement analysis + internship → Junior Financial Analyst / Finance Analyst.

This is one of the most direct and affordable routes in India.
Route B — BBA Finance
Class 12 → BBA / BBA Finance → accounting + corporate finance + Excel + internship → Corporate Finance / FP&A / Finance Analyst roles.

BBA is useful for students who prefer business and management along with finance.
Route C — Economics / Statistics / Mathematics
Class 12 → BA/B.Sc. Economics, Statistics or Mathematics → Excel + finance + data analysis → Financial Analyst / Risk / Research / Analytics roles.

This route can be especially strong for analytical finance and quantitative work.
Route D — Engineering to Finance
MPC → B.Tech/B.E. → learn accounting, finance and modelling → finance internship / MBA / CFA pathway → Financial Analyst / Business Finance / Investment roles.

Engineering graduates regularly move into finance, especially when they have strong analytical and data skills.
Higher study — MBA Finance
Bachelor's degree → CAT / XAT / CMAT / other business-school route → MBA/PGDM Finance → Financial Analyst / Corporate Finance / Investment Banking / FP&A.

An MBA can improve access to higher-level finance roles, but you do not need an MBA for every entry-level Financial Analyst job.
CFA pathway
CFA is optional, not compulsory. It is especially useful for investment analysis, equity research, portfolio-related roles and deeper valuation knowledge.

Under current CFA Institute rules, an undergraduate may register for CFA Level I when the selected exam window is 23 months or less before the graduation month, subject to the programme's other eligibility requirements.
NISM Research Analyst
Do not confuse a general Financial Analyst with a SEBI-regulated Research Analyst.

If your job involves preparing/publishing securities research under the SEBI Research Analyst framework, the relevant persons may need NISM-Series-XV: Research Analyst Certification. NISM launched a revised Series-XV exam from 20 January 2026.

This certification is not required for every corporate Financial Analyst job.

CIRCLE 3 · FINANCE CAREERS

What kind of Financial Analyst could you become?

“Financial Analyst” is an umbrella term. The actual work changes significantly between corporate finance, investment research and specialised finance teams.

Corporate Financial Analyst
Analyse a company's revenue, costs, profit and financial performance. Prepare reports and help managers understand what is happening in the business.
P&LReportsBusiness Decisions
FP&A Analyst
Financial Planning & Analysis. Build budgets, forecasts and scenario models; compare actual performance with plan and explain the difference.
Equity Research Analyst
Study listed companies, industries, financial statements, management information and valuation to form investment views. Regulated securities-research activities may have SEBI/NISM requirements.
Investment Analyst
Evaluate companies, funds, projects or other investments by analysing risk, return, valuation and market conditions.
Credit Analyst
Assess whether a person or company is likely to repay debt. Work can involve banks, NBFCs, credit-rating agencies and lending businesses.
Risk Analyst
Measure financial, market, credit or business risks and help organisations understand possible losses and controls.
Treasury Analyst
Help a company manage cash, banking relationships, liquidity, investments, debt and foreign-exchange exposure.
Valuation Analyst
Estimate what a company, business unit, asset or investment may be worth using financial models and comparable data.
Business Finance Analyst
Work closely with business teams to understand pricing, margins, growth, costs and whether business decisions make financial sense.
Finance Data Analyst
Combine finance knowledge with Excel, SQL, Power BI, Python and dashboards to automate reporting and analyse large financial datasets.
Financial Analyst vs Accountant
Accountant: records, classifies and reports financial transactions accurately.
Financial Analyst: uses financial information to understand performance, forecast the future and support decisions.

Accounting knowledge is still essential for analysts.
Financial Analyst vs CA
Financial Analyst: a job role; there is no single mandatory professional qualification.
Chartered Accountant (CA): a regulated professional qualification obtained through the ICAI pathway.

CAs can become Financial Analysts, but you do not need to be a CA for every analyst position.
Financial Analyst vs Investment Banker
Financial Analyst: broad analytical role across companies and finance teams.
Investment Banking: specialised work such as mergers, acquisitions and capital raising, often with longer hours and a different recruitment market.
Financial Analyst vs Stock Trader
Financial Analyst: analyses information and builds recommendations or business decisions.
Trader: executes or manages market positions.

Becoming a Financial Analyst does not mean spending the day buying and selling stocks.
Where you can work
Banks, fintech companies, consulting firms, manufacturing companies, IT companies, Global Capability Centres (GCCs), investment firms, insurance companies, startups, ecommerce companies, accounting/advisory firms and almost any large organisation with a finance department.
AI-era reality
AI can automate repetitive reporting, spreadsheet formulas and first-draft analysis. That increases the value of analysts who can check data quality, understand accounting, question assumptions, build scenarios and explain why a financial result matters to the business.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Financial Analyst salaries vary greatly by city, company, degree, finance speciality and skill level.

Intern / finance trainee
₹10,000 – ₹25,000/month
A practical broad range for many internships and trainee roles. Premium finance internships can pay more, while some smaller-company internships pay less.
Starting / junior analyst
₹3 – ₹6 lakh/year
A realistic broad early-career range for many B.Com/BBA/Economics graduates. Strong colleges, internships, modelling skills or specialised employers can offer more.
India benchmark
≈ ₹5.71 lakh/year
Indeed's 1 October 2026 India benchmark for Financial Analysts is about ₹5,70,628/year, based on 123 reported salaries. This mixes experience levels, so it should not be treated as a guaranteed fresher salary.
Experienced analyst
₹6 – ₹12 lakh+/year
Analysts with strong FP&A, valuation, investment research, business partnering or data/automation skills can move above basic junior finance salaries.
Senior Financial Analyst
Around ₹10 – ₹15 lakh+/year can be possible
Indeed's current India page also shows a Senior Financial Analyst benchmark above ₹11 lakh/year, but titles, companies and experience vary greatly.
High-end finance roles
Can be substantially higher
Investment banking, top equity research, private equity, high-end corporate finance and specialised global finance roles can pay much more, but they are highly competitive and should not be presented as the normal fresher outcome.
Course cost
You do not need an expensive private “Financial Analyst course” to start. A good B.Com/BBA/Economics degree plus Excel, accounting, financial modelling, internships and projects can be more valuable than collecting many certificates.
Money warningBe careful with institutes promising ₹10–20 lakh packages after a short financial-modelling course. High-paying finance jobs depend on strong fundamentals, college/employer access, internships, communication, analytical skill and often intense competition.

How to read your four circles

Numbers + business + decision-making — Financial Analysis may be a strong fit.

Love accounting, audit and taxation — compare Chartered Accountancy / Accounting.

Love markets and company valuation — explore Equity Research / Investment Analysis and possibly CFA later.

One extra rule: a good Financial Analyst does not stop at “profit increased by 10%” — they explain why it changed, whether it can continue and what the business should do next.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, start with practical money and business understanding rather than professional certifications.

1Right now, in school
  • Become strong in percentages, ratios, averages and graphs
  • Learn basic spreadsheet skills: rows, columns, formulas, totals and charts
  • Understand simple concepts such as revenue, cost, profit, assets, debt and cash
2After Class 10
  • Prefer MEC / Commerce with Mathematics if you already know you enjoy Finance
  • CEC is also possible; strengthen Maths and statistics separately
  • After Class 12, compare B.Com, BBA Finance, Economics, Statistics and other analytical degrees
3During college
  • Become genuinely good at Excel + financial statements + accounting + financial modelling
  • Build at least two projects using real company annual reports
  • Get finance internships before paying for advanced credentials; later choose CFA, MBA, NISM, SQL/Python or other skills according to your target role
Best first testTake the annual report of a well-known listed company. Find its revenue, expenses, profit and cash from operations for two years. Calculate the percentage change and write three sentences explaining what improved or became worse. If you enjoy turning those numbers into a business story, Financial Analysis may genuinely suit you.
