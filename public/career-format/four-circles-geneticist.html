Geneticist

Geneticists study genes, DNA, heredity and genetic variation. They may investigate inherited diseases, analyse genomes, improve crops and animals, study evolution, work in biotechnology or develop new diagnostic and research methods. “Geneticist” is a broad science career — a research geneticist, clinical/medical geneticist, plant geneticist and computational geneticist can have very different training routes.

CIRCLE 1

Would Genetics suit you?

Being interested in DNA is a good start. Real genetics involves laboratory work, statistics, experiments, data analysis and careful interpretation.

Do you genuinely enjoy Biology, especially cells, DNA, inheritance and evolution?

Would you enjoy laboratory work such as DNA extraction, PCR, sequencing or microscopy?

Are you willing to learn statistics and basic programming to analyse genetic data?

Can you repeat experiments carefully and accept that research may fail many times?

Would you enjoy reading scientific papers and asking why a biological result happened?

Are you open to postgraduate study if your preferred genetics career requires an M.Sc., PhD or medical specialisation?

CIRCLE 2

Which pathway can you take?

There is no single compulsory “Geneticist entrance exam.” The right route depends on whether you want research, biotechnology, agriculture, computational genetics or medical genetics.

Important termsGenetics = study of genes and heredity. Genomics = study of many genes or whole genomes. Bioinformatics = using computing and statistics to analyse biological data. PCR = Polymerase Chain Reaction, a laboratory method used to make many copies of selected DNA.
After Class 10
BiPC is the most direct route.

Biology is essential for most Genetics, Biotechnology, Life Sciences and Biomedical Science programmes. Chemistry is also very important.

PCMB is excellent if available and manageable because Mathematics helps greatly in genomics and bioinformatics.

MPC students can still enter some Biotechnology/Bioinformatics or computational routes, depending on university eligibility.
Route A — B.Sc. Genetics / Life Sciences
Class 10 → BiPC → B.Sc. Genetics / Human Genetics / Biotechnology / Biochemistry / Microbiology / Zoology / Life Sciences → M.Sc. Genetics / Human Genetics / Genomics / Biotechnology → research or industry.

This is one of the clearest routes for students who want laboratory and biological genetics.
Route B — B.Tech Biotechnology
Class 10 → suitable Class 12 science combination → B.Tech Biotechnology / related programme → genetics/genomics electives + laboratory projects → M.Tech/MS/industry/research.

Eligibility for Biotechnology engineering programmes varies by institution, so check whether the college accepts MPC, BiPC/PCB or both.
Route C — Research-first science route
BiPC/PCMB → IISER / NISER / strong B.Sc./BS programme → major in Biological Sciences → genetics/genomics research projects → Integrated PhD / PhD.

This is especially strong if your goal is to become a research geneticist rather than immediately enter a routine laboratory job.
CUET route
CUET-UG is used by many participating universities for undergraduate science programmes. In 2026, CUET includes a combined domain subject covering Biology / Biological Science / Biotechnology / Biochemistry.

But CUET is not compulsory for every Genetics course; some universities use merit or their own admission process.
Postgraduate specialisation
Useful programmes include M.Sc. Human Genetics, Genetics, Genomics, Molecular Biology, Biotechnology, Biomedical Science, Bioinformatics and Computational Biology.

As one current example, SDM University lists a 2-year M.Sc. Human Genetics for graduates from biological-science backgrounds.
Biomedical Genomics route
The National Institute of Biomedical Genomics (NIBMG) offers advanced training and research in human genetics/genomics. For 2026 it invited applications for an Integrated MS-PhD in Biotechnology with specialisation in Biomedical Genomics.

This is the type of route students may target after building a strong biological-science foundation.
PhD route
B.Sc./B.Tech → M.Sc./M.Tech or Integrated PhD → PhD in Genetics / Genomics / Molecular Biology / related field → Research Geneticist / Scientist.

For many independent research-scientist and university careers, a PhD is normally expected.
Research entrance examples
Depending on programme and institute, postgraduate/research routes may use GAT-B, BET, CSIR-UGC NET/JRF, GATE, DBT/ICMR fellowships or institute-specific tests/interviews.

There is no single exam that every future geneticist must clear.
Medical Genetics route
If you want to diagnose and medically manage patients with genetic disorders, that is a medical-specialist route.

Typical pathway: BiPC → NEET-UG → MBBS → postgraduate/superspecialty medical training in the relevant genetics pathway.

A research geneticist with B.Sc./M.Sc./PhD training is not the same as a medical doctor specialising in genetics.
You must clear
Research Geneticist: Class 10 → BiPC/PCMB → B.Sc./BS/B.Tech in Genetics/Life Sciences/Biotechnology → M.Sc./Integrated PhD → PhD → research scientist.

Industry Geneticist: Class 10 → BiPC/PCMB → relevant B.Sc./B.Tech → strong molecular/genomics skills → M.Sc. often useful → biotech/genomics laboratory.

Medical Genetics: Class 10 → BiPC → NEET-UG → MBBS → recognised specialist medical training → Medical Genetics career.
Very important“Geneticist” is not one single licensed profession. A research geneticist, genetic counsellor and medical geneticist have different qualifications and responsibilities. Never assume a Genetics degree allows someone to diagnose or treat patients as a doctor.
CIRCLE 3 · GENETICS CAREERS

What kind of Geneticist could you become?

Genes affect medicine, agriculture, evolution and biotechnology. Your specialisation determines both the science you study and the jobs available.

Human Geneticist
Study genetic variation in humans and how genes are connected with inherited traits and disease.
Human DNADiseaseGenomics
Molecular Geneticist
Study how genes function at the molecular level, including DNA replication, gene expression, mutations and regulation.
Genomics Scientist
Analyse large-scale genome data using DNA sequencing and computational methods. Modern genomics increasingly combines wet-lab biology with bioinformatics.
Cancer Geneticist / Cancer Genomics Researcher
Study mutations and genomic changes involved in cancer to improve classification, diagnosis, treatment research and precision medicine.
Population Geneticist
Study genetic variation across populations to understand ancestry, evolution, migration, natural selection and disease risk.
Plant Geneticist
Study genes controlling crop traits such as yield, disease resistance, drought tolerance and quality. Work can connect to plant breeding and agricultural biotechnology.
Animal Geneticist
Study inherited traits in livestock, poultry and other animals to support breeding, health, productivity and conservation.
Microbial Geneticist
Study genes in bacteria, fungi and other microorganisms. Applications include biotechnology, antimicrobial resistance, fermentation and infectious-disease research.
Developmental Geneticist
Study how genes control growth and development from embryo to adult organism.
Computational Geneticist
Use programming, statistics and algorithms to analyse genetic and genomic datasets.
Python / RStatisticsSequencing Data
Forensic Genetics Scientist
Use DNA analysis for identity and forensic investigations in authorised laboratories. This overlaps with Forensic Science and requires appropriate forensic/laboratory training.
Conservation Geneticist
Use genetic information to understand endangered populations, biodiversity and conservation strategies.
Gene-editing Researcher
Study technologies such as CRISPR and other gene-editing methods for basic research, biotechnology, agriculture or therapeutic research under strict scientific and ethical controls.
Medical Geneticist
A medical doctor with specialist genetics training who evaluates and manages patients/families affected by genetic disease. This is a separate medical route from B.Sc./M.Sc./PhD genetics.
Geneticist vs Genetic Counsellor
Geneticist: may be a laboratory/research scientist studying genes and genomes, or a medically trained genetics specialist depending on qualification.

Genetic Counsellor: helps individuals/families understand genetic-test results, inheritance, risk and healthcare choices using specialised counselling/genetics training. It is a distinct professional role.
Geneticist vs Biotechnologist
Geneticist: specialises in heredity, genes and genomes.
Biotechnologist: applies biological systems more broadly to create products/processes in health, agriculture, food and industry.

There is substantial overlap.
Geneticist vs Bioinformatician
Geneticist: focuses on the biological/genetic question.
Bioinformatician: specialises in computational methods for analysing biological data.

Modern genomics teams often need both skill sets, and one person may develop expertise in both.
Where you can work
Genomics and diagnostic laboratories, biotechnology companies, pharmaceutical companies, universities, research institutes, hospitals/research centres, agriculture/seed companies, forensic labs, conservation organisations and bioinformatics teams.
AI-era relevance
Genetics is becoming more computational. AI and machine learning are increasingly used to interpret genome sequences, predict variant effects, analyse gene expression and discover biological patterns. Geneticists who understand both Biology and data analysis will have stronger long-term options.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

There is no single reliable India-wide salary for “Geneticist” because jobs appear under titles such as Research Assistant, Genomics Scientist, Molecular Biologist, Bioinformatician, Scientist and Medical Geneticist.

Lab / research trainee
₹10,000 – ₹25,000/month
Internships and junior laboratory roles vary widely. Focus on gaining real molecular-biology, sequencing and data-analysis experience.
Early-career private research / genomics
₹25,000 – ₹50,000/month
A broad practical range for junior roles in molecular biology, genomics, diagnostics, biotechnology and bioinformatics. Higher-skill computational roles can differ significantly.
PhD — JRF stage
₹37,000/month + applicable HRA
Current Government of India research-fellowship norms commonly use ₹37,000/month for Junior Research Fellowship under eligible schemes.
PhD — SRF stage
₹42,000/month + applicable HRA
Eligible fellows may move to the Senior Research Fellowship level after the required assessment under the relevant scheme.
Experienced scientist / genomics specialist
₹50,000 – ₹1,00,000+/month
Experienced industry scientists, genomics specialists, bioinformatics professionals and project leaders can earn more depending on company, city, degree and technical expertise.
Medical Genetics
Different medical salary pathway
A medically trained genetics specialist follows the MBBS + postgraduate/superspecialty route, so compensation should be compared with specialist medical careers rather than research-scientist salaries.
Study cost
You do not need an expensive bachelor's degree titled exactly “Genetics.” A strong public B.Sc./BS in Life Sciences, Biotechnology, Biochemistry, Microbiology or related fields can provide an excellent foundation if it includes good laboratory training and access to research.
Money warningDo not choose a very expensive Genetics degree expecting a high-paying job immediately after B.Sc. Many true geneticist/research roles value M.Sc. or PhD-level training. Build laboratory + bioinformatics skills so your degree also opens biotechnology and data-oriented jobs.

How to read your four circles

Biology + experiments + DNA — Genetics may be a strong fit.

Love Biology but dislike laboratory/research work — compare healthcare, Nutrition, Agriculture or other life-science careers.

Love genes + computers — compare Bioinformatics / Computational Biology.

One extra rule: a Geneticist does not just study why children resemble their parents — modern genetics uses DNA, statistics and computation to understand how biological information works.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, the best preparation is strong Biology plus curiosity about experiments and data.

1Right now, in school
  • Become strong in Biology, especially cells, chromosomes, DNA and inheritance
  • Do not ignore Maths — probability and statistics become important later
  • Learn basic coding such as Python when comfortable; modern genomics uses large datasets
2After Class 10
  • Choose BiPC for the clearest genetics/life-science route; choose PCMB if you want to keep strong computational options too
  • After Class 12, compare Genetics, Biotechnology, Biochemistry, Microbiology, Life Sciences, Biomedical Science and research-oriented BS programmes
  • Shortlist universities first, then check whether they use CUET, their own entrance test or merit
3During college
  • Learn molecular biology, PCR, genetics, statistics and basic bioinformatics
  • Get laboratory/research internships and learn how to keep proper experimental records
  • By final year, decide whether you prefer research genetics, genomics/bioinformatics, agriculture genetics, biotechnology or medical genetics
Best first testTry a simple inheritance investigation using a safe dataset — for example plant traits or a simulated family pedigree. Predict the inheritance pattern, then check whether the data actually supports your prediction. If you enjoy asking “What genetic explanation fits the evidence?”, Genetics may genuinely suit you.
