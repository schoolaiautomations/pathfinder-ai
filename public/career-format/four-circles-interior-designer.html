Interior Designer

Interior Designers shape how indoor spaces look, feel and function. The career combines creativity with measurements, furniture, lighting, materials, budgets, software, client meetings and site execution. It is not only “choosing colours” — a good designer must make a space practical, buildable and affordable.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want interior design?

Do not answer based only on beautiful rooms on Instagram. Real interior design includes measurements, drawings, budgets, contractors and many client changes.

When you enter a room, do you notice layout, furniture, lighting, colour and how people move through the space?

Do you enjoy both creative ideas and practical problem-solving?

Can you measure carefully and work with dimensions, scale, floor plans and accurate drawings?

Can you accept a client saying “I don't like it” and redesign the same space several times?

Would you enjoy learning materials, furniture, lighting, plumbing/electrical coordination and how things are actually built on site?

Would you still choose this career if some days were spent on contractor calls, site problems, quotations and deadlines instead of designing?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

Interior Design is more flexible than Architecture. You usually do not need MPC or a single compulsory national entrance exam.

1Drawing & Visual Thinking
Sketching, perspective, proportion, colour, composition and visual communication help you develop and explain design ideas.
2Maths & Measurement
Interior designers constantly use dimensions, scale, areas, quantities, budgets and material calculations. Advanced Maths is not the centre of the career, but accuracy matters.
3Communication
You must understand what a client actually needs, explain ideas clearly, negotiate changes and coordinate contractors, carpenters, electricians and vendors.
After Class 10
Intermediate / Class 12 in any recognised stream — MPC, BiPC, CEC, Commerce or Humanities — can lead to Interior Design. After Class 12, you can join a B.Des., B.Sc., BA/B.Voc or diploma programme in Interior Design / Interior & Spatial Design / Furniture & Interior Design, depending on the institution.
Entrance exams
There is no single entrance exam for every Interior Design college.

NID DAT — if targeting NID's B.Des admissions. NID Ahmedabad currently offers Furniture and Interior Design as a B.Des discipline, and NID's 2026 B.Des eligibility accepts Class 12 students from any stream.

Other design colleges may use their own design aptitude test, interview, portfolio review or Class 12 merit.
Degree or diploma?
Degree route: usually gives broader design education, internships and stronger long-term progression.
Diploma route: can be faster and practical, but quality varies greatly. Check curriculum, portfolio work, software, site exposure and placements before paying.
What you must learn
Space PlanningAutoCADSketchUp / 3DMaterialsLightingFurnitureEstimationSite Execution
You must clear
Class 12 in any stream → design entrance / college admission if required → Interior Design degree or diploma → strong portfolio + CAD/3D skills → internship/site experience → Junior Interior Designer → Interior Designer.
Do not confuseInterior Design is not the same qualification as Architecture. If your goal is to use the regulated title Architect and practise architecture, that is a separate B.Arch + Council of Architecture registration pathway.
CIRCLE 3 · CAREER AREAS

What can an Interior Designer work on?

You can specialise by type of space, design skill or stage of a project.

Residential Interiors
Homes, apartments and villas. Space planning, kitchens, wardrobes, furniture, lighting, finishes and client-specific design.
HomesKitchensFurniture
Commercial Interiors
Offices, banks, clinics and business spaces. Design must support workflow, staff needs, branding, safety and efficient use of space.
Hospitality Interiors
Hotels, resorts, restaurants and cafés. Strong focus on atmosphere, guest experience, durability, lighting, materials and brand identity.
Retail Design
Stores, showrooms and shopping spaces. The designer thinks about product display, customer movement, lighting and brand experience.
Workplace Design
Corporate offices, co-working spaces and campuses. Work includes workstations, meeting rooms, collaboration areas, acoustics and employee experience.
Furniture Design
Design chairs, tables, storage, modular systems and custom furniture. Requires understanding ergonomics, materials, joinery and manufacturing.
Kitchen & Wardrobe Design
A specialised commercial route involving modular kitchens, wardrobes, hardware, ergonomics, measurements and detailed execution.
Lighting Design
Use natural and artificial light to improve function, comfort and atmosphere. Specialist lighting work often overlaps with architecture, electrical design and product specification.
Exhibition / Experience Design
Museums, exhibitions, brand displays, event spaces and temporary installations. This combines interiors, graphics, storytelling and fabrication.
3D Visualisation
Create realistic images and walkthroughs before construction. Strong skills in 3D modelling, rendering and visual communication can become a separate specialist career.
SketchUp3ds Max / BlenderEnscape / LumionRendering
Site Execution
Turn drawings into reality. Coordinate carpenters, electricians, painters, ceiling contractors, flooring teams and vendors; check dimensions, quality and timelines.
Turnkey / Design-Build
Some designers take responsibility for both design + execution. This can increase revenue, but also increases risk because the designer must control costing, purchasing, labour, quality and deadlines.
Interior Designer vs Architect
Interior Designer: focuses primarily on interior spaces, layouts, furniture, finishes, lighting and user experience.
Architect: is trained for whole buildings and the wider built environment through a recognised architecture qualification.

India's Council of Architecture states that a person using the title Architect must be registered with the Council. Interior-design training does not by itself make someone a registered Architect.
Interior Designer vs Decorator
Interior Designer: can work on space planning, technical drawings, furniture layouts, materials and site execution.
Interior Decorator: usually focuses more narrowly on styling, colours, furnishings, accessories and visual finishing.
In real projects the roles can overlap, but technical design ability makes a major difference.
Who hires
Interior design studios, architecture firms, real-estate developers, furniture/modular companies, home-interior brands, retail companies, hotel groups, workplace-design firms, construction/design-build companies and 3D visualisation studios.
Portfolio matters
Your portfolio is one of your strongest job tools. Show the problem, floor plan, sketches, material thinking, 3D views and final execution — not only pretty rendered images. Employers want to know whether you can think and execute.
Freelance / Own studio
Experienced designers often take independent residential or commercial projects. Income can improve significantly, but you must find clients, prepare quotations, control contractors, collect payments and manage mistakes.
Your Plan B
Interior-design skills can also lead into:
Furniture Design3D VisualisationRetail DesignBIM/CADSet DesignProject Coordination
RealitySoftware does not make you a designer. AutoCAD, SketchUp and rendering tools help you communicate an idea. The harder skill is creating a layout that actually solves the client's problem and can be built within budget.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Interior Design income depends strongly on portfolio, software skills, city, site experience, sales ability and whether you work for a firm or independently.

Intern
₹5,000 – ₹15,000/month
Current listings vary widely. Internship value should also be judged by real drawings, site visits, mentoring and portfolio work.
Junior Interior Designer
₹15,000 – ₹25,000/month
Indeed's 2026 India-wide benchmark is about ₹18,800/month for Junior Interior Designer, with reported salaries varying considerably by employer and city.
Interior Designer
₹20,000 – ₹45,000+/month
Indeed's current India-wide average is about ₹23,400/month. Designers with strong execution, client handling, 3D/BIM skills or larger-city experience can earn more.
Senior / Project Designer
₹35,000 – ₹80,000+/month
Senior roles can include design leadership, client meetings, site control and team management. Current job listings show a broad spread, including ₹30,000–₹70,000+ offers for experienced designers.
Freelance / Own studio
Highly variable
Independent designers may charge design fees, per-square-foot fees, consultation fees or earn margins through turnkey execution. Revenue can become much higher than salary — but so can project risk and unpaid bills.
Course cost
There is no single standard fee because Interior Design is offered through B.Des, B.Sc, BA/B.Voc and diploma programmes across public and private institutions. Private design education can become expensive. Compare total fees with studio quality, faculty, portfolio outcomes, software/site training and placements.
Ask at homeIf a course costs several lakhs, does it produce strong portfolios and real internships? Since starting salaries can be around ₹15,000–25,000/month, avoid a large education loan for a weak programme simply because the campus looks attractive.

How to read your four circles

Creative + practical + people-oriented — Interior Design may be a strong fit.

Love buildings at a larger scale — compare Architecture.

Love styling but dislike drawings/site work — Interior Decoration, Styling or Visual Merchandising may fit better.

One extra rule: the best interiors are not the most expensive-looking rooms. They solve the user's needs beautifully within real constraints.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

You can test Interior Design before paying for a course.

1Right now, in school
  • Sketch rooms and furniture from observation instead of copying only Pinterest images
  • Measure your bedroom/classroom and draw a simple floor plan to scale
  • Learn why different materials are used — plywood, laminates, tiles, stone, glass, metal, fabric and paint
2After Class 10
  • Choose any stream you enjoy and can perform well in; there is no compulsory MPC requirement for most Interior Design routes
  • Build drawing, spatial reasoning, colour/material understanding and communication
  • If targeting NID or another competitive design school, prepare for its design aptitude / studio-style selection process rather than memorising only theory
3Before choosing college
  • Compare student portfolios, not just campus photos or advertisements
  • Check whether students learn CAD/3D, materials, estimation, site execution and internships — not only decorating
  • Ask where recent graduates actually work and what their starting roles are
Best first testRedesign one real room with a fixed budget of ₹50,000. Measure it, create a floor plan, choose furniture/materials, estimate every cost and make one 3D/sketched view. If you enjoy balancing beauty + function + dimensions + budget, Interior Design may genuinely suit you.
