Chef / Culinary Professional

Chefs turn ingredients into safe, consistent and enjoyable food — but the career is much more than “loving to cook.” Professional kitchens are fast, hot, physically demanding and highly disciplined. A chef must control taste, hygiene, timing, cost, teamwork and consistency every single day.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want to become a chef?

Cooking at home and working in a professional kitchen are very different experiences.

Do you enjoy cooking enough to repeat the same dish many times until it tastes and looks consistent?

Can you work quickly and accurately when several orders arrive at the same time?

Are you comfortable standing for long hours in a hot, noisy kitchen?

Can you follow strict hygiene, food-safety and cleaning procedures every day?

Can you accept correction from a senior chef and improve your technique without taking it personally?

Would you still choose this career if you had to work nights, weekends, festivals and holidays when restaurants are busiest?

CIRCLE 2

Skills & pathway

There is no single compulsory degree or national entrance exam to become a chef. You can enter through a full hospitality degree, a culinary degree, a food-production diploma or a shorter craftsmanship route.

1Food Production
Knife skills, cooking methods, seasoning, sauces, stocks, Indian and international cuisines, bakery basics, plating and kitchen organisation.
2Food Safety & Hygiene
Temperature control, storage, cross-contamination, personal hygiene, cleaning and safe handling are professional essentials.
3Cost & Teamwork
Professional cooking is also about portions, wastage, inventory, food cost, speed and working as part of a kitchen brigade.
After Class 10
You have two broad choices.

Option 1 — Continue Class 11–12 in any stream, ideally with English, then choose a culinary / hotel-management course.

Option 2 — Skill route after Class 10: NCHMCT currently lists a Craftsmanship Certificate Course in Food Production of about 1½ years for Class 10-pass students under its current eligibility framework.
After Class 12
Common routes include:

Diploma in Food Production — NCHMCT currently lists this as a 1½-year programme for Class 12 students.

B.Sc. Hospitality & Hotel Administration — a broader 3-year degree covering Food Production, F&B Service, Front Office and Housekeeping, with management subjects.

BBA / Bachelor's in Culinary Arts — available at selected institutions for students who want a more chef-focused degree.
Entrance exams
NCHM JEE — the main national entrance for the 3-year B.Sc. Hospitality & Hotel Administration route through NCHMCT-affiliated IHMs. Current eligibility requires Class 12 with English as one of the subjects.

Diploma / Culinary programmes — many are admitted directly by the institute through merit, application or their own selection process. There is no single chef entrance exam for all colleges.
Which route is best?
If your main goal is the kitchen: Food Production / Culinary Arts gives more direct chef training.
If you want wider hotel-management options: B.Sc. HHA gives broader hospitality exposure.
If family budget/time is limited: a recognised shorter food-production course + strong kitchen experience can be a practical route.
You must clear
Class 10 → Class 12 in any stream → NCHM JEE / college admission if required → Culinary Arts / Food Production / Hotel Management training → industrial training / kitchen internship → Commis / Junior Chef → Chef.

Shorter route: Class 10 → recognised Craftsmanship Food Production course → practical training → entry-level kitchen role → experience + specialisation.
ImportantYou do not become a strong chef just by getting a certificate. Employers care about practical speed, taste, hygiene, consistency, discipline and whether you can work in a real kitchen under pressure.
CIRCLE 3 · KITCHEN CAREERS

What kind of chef could you become?

Professional kitchens have different sections and specialisations. Most chefs become strong in one area before moving into leadership.

Commis Chef
Entry-level professional kitchen role. Prepare ingredients, cook assigned items, maintain hygiene and learn one station under senior chefs.
LearningPrepKitchen basics
Chef de Partie
Runs one kitchen station or section. Examples include grill, curry, sauce, cold kitchen or bakery sections depending on the property.
Sous Chef
Second-in-command in many kitchens. Supervises staff, service, quality, prep, wastage and kitchen coordination.
Executive / Head Chef
Leads the kitchen operation: menu planning, food cost, staffing, suppliers, quality, hygiene, training and overall culinary standards.
Indian Cuisine Chef
Specialise in regional Indian cuisines, curries, tandoor, biryani, kebabs, South Indian food or other regional styles. Strong specialists can be highly valued.
Continental / Western Chef
Work with European/Western cooking methods, sauces, grills, pastas, breakfast, meat/fish preparation and plated dishes.
Asian Cuisine Chef
Chinese, Thai, Japanese, Korean and other Asian cuisines. Authentic technique and ingredient knowledge matter.
Pastry Chef
Desserts, pastry, chocolate and plated sweets. Requires precision, temperature control and strong presentation skills.
Baker
Bread, cakes, pastries and baked products. NCHMCT currently also offers a separate 1½-year Diploma in Bakery & Confectionery after Class 12.
Tandoor Chef
Specialise in tandoor cooking, breads, kebabs and high-heat techniques. This is a practical speciality with strong demand in Indian hospitality.
Banquet / Bulk Production
Prepare large quantities for weddings, conferences and hotel events while maintaining timing and consistency for hundreds or thousands of guests.
Cruise / Airline Catering
Large-scale food production under strict hygiene, timing and logistics requirements. Cruise roles may involve long contracts away from home.
Personal / Private Chef
Cook for individuals, families, executives or private events. Requires discretion, menu flexibility and direct client management.
Food Entrepreneur
Cloud kitchen, restaurant, bakery, catering or packaged-food business. Cooking skill helps, but business success also needs pricing, marketing, staff, purchasing, licences and cash-flow control.
Chef vs Cook
Cook: prepares food, often following established recipes and instructions.
Chef: usually has broader professional responsibility for technique, quality, menu, team, costing or a kitchen section.
In real workplaces the titles can overlap, especially in smaller restaurants.
Chef vs Hotel Manager
Chef: kitchen and culinary production.
Hotel Manager: coordinates the wider hotel business — rooms, front office, F&B, staff, operations and finances.
B.Sc. HHA exposes students to both, but kitchen-focused students need strong Food Production experience.
Where chefs work
Hotels, restaurants, resorts, cafés, bakeries, catering companies, cloud kitchens, hospitals/institutions, airline kitchens, cruise ships, clubs and private homes.
Career ladder
Trainee / Apprentice → Commis → Demi Chef de Partie → Chef de Partie → Sous Chef → Executive Sous Chef → Executive / Head Chef.
Titles vary by hotel and restaurant, but experience and responsibility usually increase step by step.
Your Plan B
Culinary skills can also lead into:
Food ProductionBakeryCateringFood StylingHospitality TrainingFood Business
Kitchen realityProfessional kitchens are intense. Expect heat, standing, cuts/burn risks, repetitive prep, pressure during service and late/weekend shifts. If you enjoy only relaxed home cooking, test a real commercial kitchen before choosing the career.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Chef salaries grow with experience, cuisine, city, hotel/restaurant brand and how much responsibility you can handle.

Intern / Trainee
₹5,000 – ₹12,000/month
Hotel and culinary internships can be modestly paid. The main value is real kitchen speed, discipline and exposure.
Commis / Junior Chef
₹12,000 – ₹22,000/month
Entry-level pay varies widely by city and employer. Current Indeed benchmarks show junior-chef pay around the mid-₹16,000s per month nationally.
Chef — India benchmark
≈ ₹20,000/month average
Indeed's May 2026 India-wide Chef average is about ₹19,972/month, based on hundreds of reported salaries.
Chef de Partie / Specialist
₹22,000 – ₹40,000+/month
Strong cuisine specialists, CDPs and experienced production chefs can earn more, especially in premium hotels/restaurants.
Sous Chef
₹30,000 – ₹60,000+/month
Indeed's current national benchmark is around ₹35,000/month, with large variation by property and experience.
Head / Executive Chef
₹40,000 – ₹1,00,000+/month
Leadership positions in premium hotels, restaurant groups, resorts or overseas markets can go substantially higher.
Course cost
NCHMCT's 3-year B.Sc. HHA currently estimates tuition at roughly ₹3–5 lakh total across institutes, with additional admission, examination, hostel and living costs. Shorter Food Production / Craftsmanship programmes can cost much less depending on institute.
Ask at homeIf a private culinary school costs ₹8–15 lakh or more, compare that cost with the likely first salary. A prestigious-looking kitchen classroom does not guarantee a chef job. Check internships, hotel partnerships, practical hours and real graduate placements.

How to read your four circles

Cooking + pressure + discipline — Chef may be a strong fit.

Love baking more than restaurant cooking — compare Bakery & Pastry.

Love food but dislike kitchen pressure — explore Food Technology, Nutrition, Hospitality Management or food business roles.

One extra rule: a professional chef is judged by consistency — not by making one excellent dish once.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

You can test this career at home before paying for culinary school.

1Right now, in school
  • Learn 10 basic dishes and repeat each until the result is consistent
  • Learn knife safety, kitchen hygiene, food storage and basic nutrition
  • Cook one complete meal within a fixed time and budget
2After Class 10
  • Continue Class 12 if possible; any stream can lead to culinary careers
  • If you want a shorter vocational route, compare recognised Craftsmanship Food Production programmes
  • Visit an IHM, hotel kitchen, bakery or restaurant and ask what the first two years of chef work actually look like
3After Class 12
  • Compare NCHM JEE + B.Sc. HHA, Diploma in Food Production and dedicated Culinary Arts programmes
  • Choose programmes with strong practical kitchens and industrial training
  • Before admission, check where recent graduates actually started: Commis? Management Trainee? Bakery? F&B?
Best first testCook the same dish for 10 people at the same time. Keep portions, taste, temperature and presentation consistent, finish on time and clean the kitchen afterward. If you enjoy the pressure as much as the cooking, a professional kitchen may genuinely suit you.
