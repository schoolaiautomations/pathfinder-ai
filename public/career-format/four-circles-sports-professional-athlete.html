Sports / Professional Athlete

Train your body and mind to compete at the highest level in a sport. Professional sport can bring medals, contracts, travel and recognition — but only a small percentage reach stable full-time income. The real career is years of disciplined training, competition, recovery and constant performance pressure.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want athlete life?

Do not answer based on trophies, fame or watching your favourite player. Answer based on the training required when nobody is watching.

Would you still train seriously 5–6 days a week even if you did not win a medal for months?

Can you repeat the same drills hundreds of times to improve one small part of your technique?

Can you accept losing, being benched, missing selection or being ranked below someone else — and return to training?

Are you willing to organise sleep, food, schoolwork and social life around training and competitions?

Can you listen to coaches, accept technical criticism and change your game even when you think you already know what to do?

Would you continue trying if your sport never made you famous and your income remained uncertain for several years?

CIRCLE 2

How do you become an athlete?

There is no one “Sports entrance exam.” Your performance record is your real qualification.

1Choose one primary sport
By the time training becomes serious, an athlete usually needs one main sport and a qualified coach. Playing many sports when young is useful, but elite development eventually becomes specialised.
2Train under a coach
Join a good school, club, district academy, recognised sports association, SAI centre or accredited academy where available. Good coaching is more important than buying expensive equipment.
3Compete regularly
Selections are based on timings, rankings, match performance, trials, skill tests and recognised competition results depending on the sport. You need an actual record of performance — not only practice videos.
After Class 10
There is no compulsory Intermediate stream for becoming a professional athlete. Choose MPC, BiPC, CEC, Commerce or Humanities based on your academic interests and backup career. The important part is finding a school/college schedule that allows serious training and recognised competition.
Entrance / selection
No universal entrance exam. Athlete selection normally happens through sport-specific district → state → national competitions, federation trials, rankings, scouting and assessment camps. Cricket, badminton, athletics, football, boxing, shooting and every other sport have different selection systems.
Khelo India / SAI
Promising athletes can be identified through recognised competition performance and assessment. Khelo India's current talent pathway moves athletes through Foundation → Grassroots → Intermediate → Elite. Selected athletes may train at Khelo India Accredited Academies or SAI National Centres of Excellence.
SAI centres
Sports Authority of India currently operates National Centres of Excellence, SAI Training Centres and other training centres across India. NCOEs provide specialised coaching, sports science, nutrition and high-performance support in priority disciplines. Use SAI's current centre locator to find the right discipline and centre rather than assuming every centre offers every sport.
You must clear
Choose a sport → train under a qualified coach → district/recognised competitions → state level → national championships/Khelo India/federation pathway → elite academy/SAI/NCOE or professional club/team selection → national/international/professional competition.

The exact ladder changes by sport.
ImportantAP PECET is not the entrance exam to become an athlete. AP PECET is for admission to D.P.Ed. and B.P.Ed. courses in Andhra Pradesh — useful for Physical Education / coaching-related careers. Athlete selection is based mainly on sporting performance and sport-specific trials.
CIRCLE 3 · THE SPORTS

Which sport can become your career?

The pathway, peak age, training environment and earning potential are different for every sport.

Cricket
One of India's largest professional sports markets. Progress through school/club cricket, district and state association age-group teams, domestic cricket and professional/franchise opportunities.
Roles: Batter, Bowler, Wicketkeeper, All-rounder.

Badminton / Tennis / Table Tennis
Ranking and tournament-driven sports. Strong coaching, regular competition, travel and individual performance are essential. Badminton has a particularly strong ecosystem in India and Hyderabad.
Athletics
Performance is measurable. Sprints, distance running, jumps, throws and combined events progress through qualifying marks, timings/distances and championships.

Football
Academy and club pathway with district/state competitions, youth leagues and professional clubs. Team selection depends heavily on match performance, position and scouting.
Hockey
School/academy → state → national pathway with a strong SAI and federation ecosystem. Professional and institutional teams can provide further opportunities.
Kabaddi / Kho-Kho
Traditional Indian team sports with school, university, state and national pathways. Professional leagues have increased visibility and earning opportunities for top selected athletes.
Volleyball / Basketball
School, academy, district, university, state and club pathways. Height can help in some positions, but skill, movement, game awareness and training quality remain essential.
Boxing / Wrestling / Judo / Taekwondo
Combat sports. Athletes compete in weight categories and progress through bouts, championships and federation selection. Strength, technique, conditioning and recovery are critical.
Weightlifting
Technique + strength sport with weight categories and performance measured through competition lifts. India has a strong SAI high-performance pathway in the discipline.
Shooting / Archery
Precision sports. Require technique, mental control and repeated competition scores. Equipment and access to qualified ranges/coaching can make entry more expensive than field sports.
Swimming / Aquatics
Training often begins young and requires regular pool access. Progress is measurable through official timings and championships.
Cycling / Rowing / Canoeing
High-endurance Olympic sports with specialised equipment/facilities. SAI NCOEs support several of these disciplines.
Para Sports
Professional and elite pathways also exist for athletes with eligible impairments. Classification is sport-specific. Khelo India and SAI include para-athlete development, and India has pathways into Para Athletics, Para Badminton, Para Shooting, Para Powerlifting and other Paralympic disciplines.
Team sport
Your performance matters, but selection also depends on position, tactics, team needs and coach decisions. Examples: Cricket, Football, Hockey, Kabaddi, Volleyball.
Individual sport
Rankings, times, distances, scores, weight lifted or direct competition results often make performance easier to measure. Examples: Athletics, Badminton, Shooting, Swimming, Weightlifting.
Professional vs sports degree
Professional Athlete: earns opportunities through competition performance.
B.P.Ed / D.P.Ed: education pathway for Physical Education and related careers.
Sports Science: separate careers such as physiotherapy, strength & conditioning, nutrition, biomechanics and sports psychology require their own qualifications.

CIRCLE 4

How will you earn?

There is no meaningful “average athlete salary.” Income varies enormously by sport, performance level, contracts, prizes, sponsorships and whether you reach a professional league or national team.

School / district athlete
Usually ₹0 salary
At this stage, families often spend money on coaching, equipment, travel, nutrition and competitions. Do not treat school medals as income.
State / developing athlete
Income often irregular
Some athletes receive scholarships, institutional support, prize money or sports-quota opportunities; many still need family support while trying to reach national level.
Khelo India Athlete
≈ ₹10,000/month OPA if selected
Khelo India's current athlete guidance describes an out-of-pocket allowance of about ₹10,000/month, plus training support of up to ₹5 lakh per athlete per year for selected athletes. This is athlete-development support, not a permanent salary.
Professional / elite athlete
No standard range
Contracts, match fees, league salaries, tournament prizes and sponsorship can range from modest amounts to very high incomes. Top cricket or league stars are extreme outliers and should never be used as the expected salary for a student choosing sport.
Government support
Outstanding athletes may become eligible for sport-specific cash awards, scholarships and recruitment opportunities under central/state policies. Andhra Pradesh's Sports Policy 2024–29, for example, provides notified cash awards for recognised medal performances — including awards at National Games and Khelo India events. Prize money is not a monthly salary.
The cost
Costs depend completely on the sport. Running may need relatively little equipment; tennis, shooting, swimming or international competition can become expensive. Budget for coaching + equipment + competition fees + travel + nutrition + physiotherapy/recovery. Ask the coach for a realistic annual budget before committing.
Ask at homeCan the family support training and travel for several years if the athlete earns nothing? What is the academic Plan B? What happens after injury or non-selection? A serious athlete needs both a performance plan and an education plan.

How to read your four circles

Love training + already competing strongly — take the athlete pathway seriously and seek higher-level coaching.

Love the sport but hate training — enjoy it recreationally; professional sport may not fit.

Performance is not yet competitive — keep training, but maintain education and a second career option.

One extra rule: professional sport is decided by performance, not passion alone. Passion keeps you training; measurable performance moves you to the next level.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, the goal is not “become famous.” The goal is to find out how good you can become with structured training.

1Next 30 days
  • Choose your primary sport and get assessed by a qualified coach — not only a friend or relative
  • Record a baseline: timing, score, ranking, match statistics, technique assessment or sport-specific test
  • Create a weekly schedule balancing school + training + sleep + recovery
2Next 6–12 months
  • Enter recognised school, district, association or federation competitions instead of playing only informal matches
  • Track your performance every month and compare it with district/state standards for your age group
  • Use the SAI centre locator and Khelo India/recognised federation pathways to identify stronger coaching and competitions if your results justify it
3When you reach state level
  • Build a genuine competition record with certificates, timings/rankings, match statistics and coach references
  • Explore SAI Training Centres, NCOEs, Khelo India assessments, state academies and professional club/federation trials relevant to your sport
  • Keep Class 12 and a degree/skill pathway active unless you already have a very strong professional pathway — do not abandon education simply because you won one tournament
Best first testTrain consistently for 90 days under a qualified coach and compete at least once. Compare your before-and-after performance. If you still love the daily training and your performance is moving toward district/state level, professional sport deserves serious exploration.
