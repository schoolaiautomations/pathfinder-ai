CIRCLE 1

Do you really want it?

Many students want the uniform, travel and salary — not the responsibility and routine behind the cockpit door. Tap your honest answer.

Can you follow checklists and procedures exactly, even when the same steps feel repetitive?

Can you stay calm and think clearly when something unexpected happens and other people are depending on you?

Are you willing to study Maths, Physics, weather, navigation, aircraft systems and regulations seriously — not just learn how to “drive” a plane?

Can you handle early mornings, night flights, weekends, changing rosters and being away from home?

Can you accept strict simulator checks, medical examinations, instructor criticism and repeated testing throughout your career?

Are you prepared to relocate for training or airline base allocation instead of expecting to work only in Andhra Pradesh?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & skills

What to focus on in school, and which road to take after Class 10.

1Maths
Speed, distance, time, fuel, headings, navigation and flight planning all use numerical thinking. You do not need olympiad-level maths, but accuracy matters.
2Physics
Aerodynamics, pressure, weather, engines, instruments and aircraft performance make much more sense when your Physics basics are strong.
3English
Aviation communication, manuals, examinations, radio calls and airline training use English. Clear listening and speaking are safety skills, not just interview skills.
After Class 10
Intermediate MPC (Maths, Physics, Chemistry) — this is the simplest route. After Class 12, you can join a DGCA-approved flying school and work toward a Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL). There is no single national entrance exam for all pilot schools; institutes such as IGRUA and airline cadet programmes may conduct their own selection tests.

THE LONGER ROAD — QUALIFICATIONS YOU WILL NEED LATER
10+2 eligibility
Pass Class 12 / Intermediate with Physics and Mathematics from a recognized board or equivalent. Do not assume any 12th stream automatically qualifies you for CPL examinations.
Medical fitness
Get DGCA medical fitness checked before committing major money. The professional route ultimately requires a valid Class 1 medical. Many aspirants begin with Class 2 and then complete Class 1 as they progress; follow the current DGCA medical process and approved examiners.
Entrance exams
No single entrance exam is compulsory for every Commercial Pilot course. IGRUA currently uses an online entrance test, Pilot Aptitude Test and interview. Airline cadet programmes and some flying schools can have their own aptitude, psychometric, English, Maths/Physics and interview rounds.
DGCA exams
After Class 12, obtain your DGCA Computer Number and clear the required pilot theory examinations in subjects such as Air Regulation, Aviation Meteorology, Air Navigation and Technical subjects, along with the applicable radio-telephony requirement.
Flying training
Train only at a DGCA-approved Flying Training Organisation (FTO). A conventional CPL route builds the required flying experience; IGRUA’s current training pathway reaches 200 hours, including single-engine, multi-engine, instrument and other required training before CPL application.
CPL + ratings
Complete the requirements for a Commercial Pilot Licence with Instrument Rating and Multi-Engine training/endorsement as applicable. Airline recruitment can then require additional aircraft-specific training or a type rating.
Airline cockpit
CPL → airline/cadet/JFO selection → aircraft type rating as required → simulator and company training → line training → First Officer. Exact sequence and who pays for each stage depends on the airline or programme.
You must clear
Class 12 with Maths & Physics → DGCA medical → DGCA theory + radio exams → about 200 hours of approved flying training → CPL + Instrument Rating + Multi-Engine → airline selection / type rating.
Do firstBefore paying lakhs to any academy, clear your education eligibility and get your DGCA medical checked. Then verify the FTO’s current DGCA approval, aircraft availability, instructor strength, safety record, published training time and DGCA ranking/performance information. A glossy brochure is not enough.
CIRCLE 3 · THE RISK

How often are the jobs?

This is the circle almost nobody checks — and for pilots it matters because the training investment is unusually high.

Who hires
Scheduled airlines such as IndiGo and the Air India group, other domestic and regional airlines, cargo operators, charter/corporate aviation companies and flying schools. Pilot jobs are national — your airline base may be Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai or elsewhere rather than your home state.
How often
Airlines recruit in batches based on fleet growth, aircraft deliveries, training capacity and operational demand. In 2026, major airlines have active pilot, trainee and cadet pathways, but hiring is not a guaranteed continuous pipeline for every fresh CPL holder.
How many
Competition changes with the cycle. Some openings are for fresh or junior pilots; many others demand a specific type rating, current Class 1 medical, radio licences, validated eGCA records and prior hours on that aircraft. A CPL is the licence to compete — not a guaranteed airline seat.
CarefulYou can finish CPL training and still wait for airline selection. During that wait, ratings and medicals must stay current and loan EMIs may already have started. Do not calculate your return on investment assuming the first airline salary begins immediately after your last flying hour.

CIRCLE 4

What you will earn

Pilot salaries can become very high — but first compare them with the cost, debt and possible waiting period.

After CPL / first airline role
Rs 0 while waiting; roughly Rs 1,00,000 – 2,50,000/month once hired
A fresh CPL may have no airline income during the job-search or additional-training period. Trainee/Junior First Officer pay varies greatly by airline, aircraft, training status and flying allowance.
First / Senior First Officer
Rs 2,50,000 – 5,00,000/month
Typical progression after gaining airline experience and line hours. Actual CTC and take-home depend on airline, fleet, seniority and flying hours.
Captain & senior roles
Rs 6,00,000 – 10,00,000+/month
Experienced narrow-body captains can reach this range; senior, training or wide-body captains can earn more. These are destination salaries, not fresher salaries.
The cost before that
Pilot training is one of the most expensive career entries in India. IGRUA’s published 2026 ab-initio CPL course with Instrument Rating and multi-engine endorsement is Rs 55 lakh. It separately lists about Rs 2.5 lakh for uniform, study material, navigation computer, headphones and related items, plus DGCA/RTR examination and licence fees, hostel and messing. A type rating or cadet-programme stage can add a substantial extra amount depending on aircraft, provider and airline arrangement.
Ask at homeCan our family carry this cost — or the education loan EMI — if airline hiring takes longer than expected? What happens financially if I need extra flying hours, repeat training, change FTO, pay for a type rating, or temporarily lose medical fitness? Build the worst-case budget before signing any loan.

How to read your four circles

All four right — go ahead with full commitment, starting today, not after results.

Any one wrong — stop and think twice before you decide.

Two or more wrong — you are choosing this career for the wrong reasons. Talk to your counsellor.

For this career, add one extra rule: never borrow a huge amount just because someone “guarantees” an airline job. Verify the medical, FTO, training contract, refund rules, type-rating responsibility and actual placement pathway yourself.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

If the four circles came out right, this is your road. Test your medical and academic fit before spending on coaching or flying.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen Class 8–10 Maths, Physics and English — especially speed-distance-time, ratios, basic mechanics, graphs and clear spoken English
  • Build disciplined sleep, fitness and study habits; aviation rewards consistency more than last-minute preparation
  • Learn what pilots actually do: read about checklists, weather, navigation and cockpit teamwork instead of watching only travel-vlog content
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC / Class 11–12 Maths + Physics if possible and aim for strong marks, especially if you may target airline cadet programmes with their own higher percentage cut-offs
  • Before paying for a flying course, complete the appropriate DGCA medical process early and confirm you are medically fit for the professional route
  • Research at least three DGCA-approved FTOs using current approval, aircraft, instructor numbers, training completion time, safety information, weather/base conditions and total written cost
3After Class 12
  • Obtain your DGCA Computer Number and prepare systematically for the required DGCA ground examinations and radio-telephony requirements
  • Choose between a conventional CPL route and an airline cadet programme only after comparing the full cost, training location, financing, type-rating inclusion, refund terms and what happens if you fail a stage
  • Complete flying training, CPL + IR + Multi-Engine requirements, then apply broadly for airline, trainee and junior pilot pathways — do not depend on one airline or one promised placement
Best first testBefore spending Rs 1 lakh, try to clear three things: 10+2 Maths/Physics plan, DGCA medical suitability, and a realistic family budget. If those three are not clear, do not rush into a pilot academy.

This is career guidance, not an official DGCA, airline, medical or salary notice. Licensing rules, medical procedures, examination requirements, FTO approvals, fees, vacancies and pay can change. Confirm the latest details directly with DGCA, the relevant airline and the approved training organisation before applying, borrowing money or paying any fee.
