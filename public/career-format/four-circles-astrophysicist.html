Astrophysicist

Astrophysicists use Physics and Mathematics to understand stars, planets, galaxies, black holes, gravitational waves, dark matter and the Universe itself. Much of the work is not simply looking through a telescope — it involves coding, analysing large datasets, building mathematical models, using simulations and testing ideas against observations.

Do you really want Astrophysics?

CIRCLE 2

Which pathway can you take?

The safest route is to build a very strong foundation in Physics and Mathematics first, then specialise in Astronomy/Astrophysics during higher study and research.

1Physics
Mechanics, electricity and magnetism, waves, optics, thermodynamics, quantum physics and relativity form the scientific foundation of astrophysics.
2Mathematics
Astrophysics uses algebra, calculus, differential equations, vectors, matrices, probability and statistics to describe physical systems.
3Programming
Python is widely useful for scientific computing and data analysis. Researchers also use numerical methods, simulations, Linux and specialised scientific software.
After Class 10
Choose MPC — Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry.

This is the strongest and safest route because serious undergraduate Physics programmes require strong Mathematics and Physics foundations. For example, IISc's 2026 B.S. (Research) programme requires Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics in Class 12.
Route A — B.Sc. Physics
Class 10 → MPC → B.Sc. Physics / B.Sc. (Hons.) Physics → M.Sc. Physics / Astronomy / Astrophysics → PhD in Astronomy/Astrophysics → Astrophysicist.

This is one of the most direct and affordable routes through Indian universities and degree colleges.
Route B — IISER
MPC → IISER Aptitude Test (IAT) → 5-year BS-MS Dual Degree → Physics major / research projects → PhD in Astronomy/Astrophysics.

IISERs are especially useful for students who want an undergraduate education strongly connected to scientific research.
Route C — NISER / CEBS
MPC → National Entrance Screening Test (NEST) → 5-year Integrated M.Sc. at NISER or UM-DAE CEBS → Physics specialisation → PhD / astrophysics research.

NEST 2026 requires students to have studied at least three of Biology, Chemistry, Mathematics and Physics in Classes 11–12.
Route D — IISc
MPC → JEE Advanced or IISER Aptitude Test (IAT) → 4-year B.S. (Research) at IISc → Physics major / research → M.S./PhD or direct research pathway.

For 2026, IISc admits Indian students to B.S. (Research) through JEE Advanced 2026 or IAT 2026, subject to eligibility.
Route E — Engineering / Engineering Physics
MPC → JEE / engineering entrance → Engineering Physics / ECE / CSE / Mechanical / related field → M.Sc./M.Tech/MS or research in astronomy, instrumentation, computation or space science → PhD where needed.

This route can be especially useful for astronomical instrumentation, detectors, telescopes, scientific computing and space-technology research.
After Bachelor's / Master's
For professional astrophysics research, students usually move into M.Sc./Integrated PhD/PhD programmes in Physics, Astronomy or Astrophysics.

Common national screening routes include JEST (Joint Entrance Screening Test), CSIR-UGC NET/JRF, GATE and institute-specific tests/interviews.
IUCAA route
IUCAA = Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics, Pune.

IUCAA is one of India's major astrophysics research centres. Its current PhD routes include INAT (IUCAA National Admission Test), JEST and CSIR-UGC NET/JRF, followed by the institute's selection process. IUCAA's INAT also supports selection to its joint M.Sc. Physics with Astrophysics programme with Savitribai Phule Pune University.
Do you need a PhD?
For a long-term career as an independent Astrophysicist / research scientist, usually yes.

A PhD trains you to do original research and is normally expected for permanent research scientist, faculty and advanced astrophysics positions. Bachelor's/Master's graduates can still work in scientific programming, observatory support, data analysis, instrumentation and project roles.
Complete roadmap
Class 10 → MPC → B.Sc./BS/BS-MS/B.Tech with strong Physics & Maths → research projects → M.Sc./Integrated PhD where applicable → JEST / NET-JRF / GATE / INAT or institute route → PhD in Astronomy/Astrophysics → Postdoctoral Research → Astrophysicist / Scientist / Faculty.
ImportantDo not choose this career only because you like space. The day-to-day work is mostly Physics, Mathematics, programming, data analysis, scientific writing and research — not watching stars through a telescope every night.
CIRCLE 3 · RESEARCH AREAS

What kind of Astrophysicist could you become?

Astrophysics contains many specialities. Researchers usually become experts in a narrow scientific area.

Observational Astrophysicist
Use telescope and satellite data to study astronomical objects. Work includes planning observations, reducing data, analysing signals and interpreting results.
TelescopesDataObservations
Theoretical Astrophysicist
Use mathematics and physical theory to explain how stars, black holes, galaxies and the Universe behave.
Computational Astrophysicist
Build computer simulations of stars, galaxies, black holes, cosmic structure and other systems. Strong programming and numerical methods are important.
Cosmologist
Study the origin, evolution and large-scale structure of the Universe, including dark matter, dark energy and the early Universe.
Stellar Astrophysicist
Study how stars form, evolve, pulsate, explode and end as white dwarfs, neutron stars or black holes.
Galactic / Extragalactic Astronomer
Study the Milky Way, other galaxies, galaxy formation, active galactic nuclei and large-scale cosmic structures.
High-Energy Astrophysicist
Study extreme objects and energetic events such as neutron stars, black holes, X-ray binaries, gamma-ray bursts and energetic cosmic phenomena.
Solar Physicist
Study the Sun, its magnetic fields, solar atmosphere, flares and space-weather effects. Indian missions such as Aditya-L1 support this area of research.
Gravitational-Wave Astronomer
Use gravitational-wave observations and theory to study merging black holes, neutron stars and fundamental physics. LIGO-India is an important future Indian facility for this field.
Planetary / Exoplanet Scientist
Study planets inside and outside our Solar System, their atmospheres, formation and possible habitability.
Radio Astronomer
Study the Universe using radio waves from facilities such as the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (GMRT) and other radio observatories.
Astronomical Instrumentation Scientist
Design and operate telescopes, spectrographs, cameras, detectors and scientific instruments. Physics, optics, electronics and engineering skills are valuable.
Astroinformatics / Data Science
Use statistics, machine learning and large-scale computing to analyse enormous astronomical datasets. Modern sky surveys produce far more data than humans can inspect manually.
Astronomer vs Astrophysicist
Astronomer: broad term for a scientist who studies objects and phenomena beyond Earth.
Astrophysicist: especially emphasises using Physics and Mathematics to explain how those objects and phenomena work.

In modern research, the two titles often overlap heavily.
Astrophysicist vs Astronaut
Astrophysicist: scientist studying the Universe through research, data, theory and observations.
Astronaut: person trained to travel/work in space missions.

Becoming an Astrophysicist does not automatically lead to becoming an astronaut.
Astrophysicist vs ISRO Engineer
Astrophysicist: primarily studies scientific questions about the Universe.
Space Engineer: designs spacecraft, instruments, structures, propulsion, electronics and mission systems.

They can work together on the same mission but follow different career paths.
Where you can work
IUCAA, Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), ARIES, TIFR, IISc, IISERs, NISER, PRL, IITs, universities, observatories, ISRO-linked research projects and international research institutions.
Current Indian research
Indian astrophysics researchers participate in major projects such as AstroSat, Aditya-L1, GMRT, LIGO-India, Thirty Meter Telescope and international sky-survey collaborations. Research increasingly combines observations with high-performance computing and AI/ML.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Astrophysics is usually a long research pathway. Early years often involve fellowships rather than a conventional company salary.

During PhD — JRF
₹37,000/month + applicable HRA
Current UGC/CSIR research-fellowship norms list Junior Research Fellowship (JRF) at ₹37,000/month for the initial period, subject to scheme/institute rules.
During PhD — SRF
₹42,000/month + applicable HRA
After the required progress assessment, eligible researchers may move to Senior Research Fellowship (SRF) at ₹42,000/month under current fellowship norms.
Research Associate / Postdoc
₹58,000 – ₹67,000/month + applicable HRA
Current CSIR-sponsored research norms list Research Associate slabs of approximately ₹58,000, ₹61,000 and ₹67,000/month, depending on qualification and experience. Institute-specific postdoctoral fellowships can differ.
Project research role
Around ₹30,000 – ₹37,000+/month in some current projects
For example, a 2026 ARIES astronomy-related Project Associate-I role offered ₹37,000 + HRA with valid GATE/NET and ₹30,000 + HRA without it. Project roles are temporary and vary by funding scheme.
Government research scientist
Can begin around Pay Level 10
A Scientist-B example from ARIES uses Level 10: ₹56,100–₹1,77,500. Permanent scientist/faculty appointments are competitive and may require a PhD, postdoctoral work and a strong publication record.
Private / transferable skills
Astrophysics graduates with strong programming, statistics and numerical skills can also move into data science, software, quantitative analysis, scientific computing, AI/ML and instrumentation, where compensation follows those industries rather than astronomy pay scales.
Education cost
You do not need an expensive private “space science” degree. Strong public programmes in Physics, IISERs, NISER, IISc, IITs and universities can provide excellent foundations. For this career, research quality matters far more than a flashy degree title.
Money warningDo not choose Astrophysics because you expect a very high salary immediately after bachelor's. The professional research route is long and highly competitive. Choose it because you genuinely enjoy Physics and research, while building coding/data skills that also give you strong Plan B options.

How to read your four circles

Physics + Maths + deep curiosity — Astrophysics may be a strong fit.

Love space but prefer building machines — compare Aerospace / Space Engineering.

Love space images but dislike advanced Maths — compare Astronomy Communication, Design, Media or other space-sector careers.

One extra rule: an Astrophysicist does not study space by imagination — they use Physics, Mathematics and evidence to test how the Universe actually works.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, the best preparation is not memorising planet facts. Build Physics, Maths and scientific thinking.

1Right now, in school
  • Become strong in Mathematics and Physics, especially algebra, graphs, motion, forces and basic electricity
  • Start basic Python when comfortable
  • Use free astronomy software or public space data to learn constellations, planets and real observations
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC
  • Compare B.Sc. Physics, IISER BS-MS, NISER Integrated M.Sc., IISc B.S. Research and Physics/Engineering Physics programmes
  • Prepare for IAT, NEST, JEE or university admission depending on your target institutes
3During college
  • Learn calculus, linear algebra, classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum physics, statistics and Python
  • Join astronomy/physics research projects and summer internships
  • Learn to read scientific papers and work with real observational or simulation data
Best first testChoose one real question such as “How do we know the temperature of a star?” Instead of reading only the final answer, learn the Physics behind spectra, find a real stellar spectrum or dataset and try to explain what evidence scientists use. If the explanation excites you more than the space picture itself, Astrophysics may genuinely suit you.
