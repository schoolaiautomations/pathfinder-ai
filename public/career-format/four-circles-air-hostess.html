Air Hostess / Cabin Crew

Cabin Crew keep passengers safe, calm and comfortable before, during and after a flight. “Air Hostess” is an older term usually used for women; airlines now commonly use Cabin Crew or Flight Attendant, and both women and men can work in the profession depending on airline hiring requirements.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want cabin-crew life?

Do not choose it only because you like travel, uniforms or airports. The real job is safety + service under pressure.

Can you stay calm and professional when a passenger is angry, sick, afraid or refusing instructions?

Can you communicate clearly in English and Hindi and speak confidently with strangers?

Can you follow strict safety procedures exactly even when the flight is busy or delayed?

Are you comfortable working early mornings, nights, weekends and holidays instead of a fixed 9-to-5 schedule?

Can you remain well-groomed, punctual and energetic after standing and working for many hours?

Would you still choose this career if most flights felt like work rather than “free travel”?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

The route is simpler than many students expect. You do not need a pilot licence, aviation degree or national entrance examination.

1English & Hindi
Major Indian airlines currently expect strong spoken communication. Air India and IndiGo both specifically mention English and Hindi communication in their cabin-crew eligibility.
2Customer Service
Learn to listen, explain, solve complaints and stay respectful. Hospitality experience can help, but airlines may hire fresh Class 12 candidates without previous customer-service employment.
3Fitness & Grooming
Airlines apply medical, height/weight, appearance and grooming requirements because crew must perform safety duties inside an aircraft. Exact criteria differ by airline.
After Class 10
Intermediate / Class 12 in any recognised stream — MPC, BiPC, CEC, Commerce or Humanities — can lead to Cabin Crew. Focus especially on English, communication, confidence and general fitness.
Entrance exam
There is no national “Air Hostess entrance exam.” You apply directly to an airline when it advertises Cabin Crew vacancies. Each airline runs its own application, assessment/interview, medical and training process.
Current airline examples
Air India: current fresher Cabin Crew eligibility includes Indian nationality with valid passport, age 18–27, Class 12 with at least 50%, English/Hindi communication, airline-specified height/BMI/vision and grooming criteria.

IndiGo: its current fresher Cabin Attendant Grade Training page lists female Indian nationals aged 18–27, minimum Class 12, English/Hindi fluency, minimum 155 cm height and airline grooming/appearance criteria.
These are examples, not universal rules. Always check the airline's current vacancy before applying.
Do I need a cabin-crew course?
No private cabin-crew diploma is universally compulsory. Air India and IndiGo currently recruit eligible candidates directly. After selection, the airline provides the required professional cabin-crew training. A hospitality/aviation course may improve confidence or service skills, but it does not guarantee airline selection.
Training
Selected candidates undergo airline training in areas such as aircraft safety, emergency procedures, evacuation, first aid, firefighting, dangerous goods awareness, security, passenger handling and service procedures. DGCA regulates cabin-safety and cabin-crew training requirements for operators.
You must clear
Class 12 in any stream → meet the airline's age/height/medical/language/document criteria → online application / airline assessment → interview → medical check → airline cabin-crew training → successfully complete required checks → Cabin Crew / Flight Attendant.
Scam warningDo not pay someone who promises a guaranteed airline Cabin Crew job. Apply through the airline's official careers website. A private “Air Hostess Academy” cannot guarantee selection by Air India, IndiGo or another airline.
CIRCLE 3 · THE JOB

What does Cabin Crew actually do?

Serving food and drinks is only one visible part of the job. Safety is the most important responsibility.

Before take-off
Attend crew briefing, check emergency/safety equipment, prepare the cabin, verify supplies, welcome passengers and help ensure baggage and seating comply with safety rules.
Safety demonstration
Explain seat belts, exits, oxygen masks, life jackets and other safety procedures, and ensure passengers follow instructions before take-off and landing.
Emergency response
The core responsibility. Cabin Crew train for evacuation, fire/smoke, decompression, turbulence, first aid, medical emergencies and other abnormal situations.
Passenger service
Help passengers with seating, meals, special assistance, questions, comfort and complaints while maintaining safety and airline service standards.
Special passengers
Support children, elderly passengers, persons with disabilities, nervous flyers and passengers needing medical or mobility assistance according to airline procedures.
International flying
International crew may experience longer sectors, immigration/customs processes, time-zone changes and layovers. Travel opportunities increase, but fatigue and time away from home also increase.
Cabin Crew vs Pilot
Cabin Crew: passenger safety, emergency response and in-flight service.
Pilot: operates and navigates the aircraft and requires DGCA pilot licensing/training.
These are completely different career pathways.
Cabin Crew vs Ground Staff
Cabin Crew: works onboard aircraft and flies with passengers.
Ground Staff: works at airports in check-in, boarding, baggage, customer service, ramp/operations or other ground roles. The recruitment and training are separate.
Career progression
Air India's current career page describes progression from Crew → Senior Cabin Crew → Cabin Executive → Cabin Manager. Airlines may use different rank names, and experienced crew can later move into training, recruitment, safety, standards or ground-management roles.
Where you may be based
Airlines assign crew to operational bases such as Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai or other hubs depending on network requirements. Be prepared to relocate. Air India's current eligibility explicitly requires willingness to be based at its domestic hubs.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Cabin-crew compensation varies widely by airline, flying hours, domestic/international routes, allowances, experience and rank.

Starting / early career
₹25,000 – ₹45,000+/month
A reasonable broad starting expectation for many Indian airline roles, but actual offers can be below or above this depending on airline, training status and flying allowances.
India benchmark
≈ ₹40,500/month average
Indeed's May 2026 India-wide Flight Attendant benchmark was about ₹40,503/month, based on a small sample of 17 reported salaries, so treat it as a rough reference rather than a guaranteed salary.
Air India benchmark
≈ ₹38,000/month reported
Indeed's current Air India aviation page reports roughly ₹37,870/month for Flight Attendant based on a small number of reported salaries. Another Air India salary page shows about ₹6.43 lakh/year using a different sample, illustrating how variable crowdsourced salary data can be.
Senior crew
₹50,000 – ₹80,000+/month
Senior Cabin Crew, pursers/cabin executives and international flying can pay more. The actual structure depends heavily on the airline.
International airlines
Some overseas airlines can offer substantially higher compensation and benefits, but recruitment is highly competitive and may require relocation abroad. Do not assume an international-airline salary when evaluating an entry-level Indian Cabin Crew career.
Course cost
You do not need to spend several lakhs on a private Air Hostess course just to become eligible. Major airlines recruit Class 12 candidates directly. Spend money on a course only if its training itself is valuable to you — not because someone promises airline placement.
Ask before payingIs this academy actually required by the airline? Does it have independently verifiable placements? What exact airline hired its students? If the airline allows direct Class 12 applications, expensive pre-training may have weak return on investment.

How to read your four circles

People + calmness + discipline + travel lifestyle — Cabin Crew may fit you.

Love aviation but dislike customer service — explore Pilot, Aircraft Maintenance, Airport Operations or Aviation Management.

Want only travel and glamour — think again; those are side effects of the job, not the job itself.

One extra rule: the best Cabin Crew member is not the person who looks best in uniform — it is the person passengers can depend on when something goes wrong.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

A school student does not need an “Air Hostess course.” Build communication, confidence and professional habits first.

1Right now, in school
  • Improve spoken English and Hindi by speaking clearly rather than trying to copy a foreign accent
  • Practise introducing yourself confidently, listening carefully and handling disagreements politely
  • Build regular fitness, healthy sleep and neat personal grooming habits
2After Class 10
  • Choose any recognised stream you can perform well in — Cabin Crew does not require MPC or BiPC
  • Complete Class 12 and build communication through debates, school events, volunteering, hospitality or customer-facing experience
  • If international flying interests you, obtain and maintain a valid passport when appropriate
3After Class 12
  • Check official airline career pages for current age, height, passport, language, medical and grooming criteria
  • Apply directly to airlines rather than assuming a private academy is compulsory
  • Prepare for airline assessment/interview: communication, customer scenarios, confidence, teamwork, grooming and knowledge of the airline
Best first testImagine a passenger is angry because a flight is delayed. Give yourself 60 seconds to respond calmly: listen, acknowledge the frustration, explain what you know, avoid making false promises and offer the next useful step. If you enjoy solving people problems while staying composed, Cabin Crew may genuinely suit you.
