Government Jobs

Work for the Central Government, Andhra Pradesh Government, Railways, public institutions or government technical departments. “Government Job” is not one career and there is no single exam — the correct route depends on whether you finish Class 10, Class 12, Diploma/ITI, Graduation or a professional degree.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want a government job?

Do not choose it only because someone says “job security.” Think about the work and the competition too.

Can you prepare consistently for competitive exams for months without knowing whether you will be selected?

Are you comfortable with work that may involve rules, files, public service, administration, documentation or routine procedures?

Can you accept being posted in a district, city or state that is not your first choice?

Are you willing to study General Awareness, Maths, Reasoning, English/Telugu and Computer skills repeatedly for competitive exams?

Would you accept a moderate starting salary if the role gives you structured pay, allowances and long-term stability?

Can you keep a degree, skill or private-sector Plan B while preparing instead of waiting years for only one notification?

CIRCLE 2

Which route should you take?

The biggest mistake is asking “Which government exam?” before deciding what qualification and type of job you want.

1Maths & Reasoning
Percentages, ratios, arithmetic, logical reasoning and data interpretation appear in many SSC, Railway, Banking and state recruitment exams.
2English / Telugu
Comprehension, grammar, vocabulary and communication matter in many competitive exams. Andhra Pradesh recruitments can also require Telugu/language knowledge depending on the post.
3General Awareness
Current affairs, Indian polity, history, geography, economy, science and basic computer knowledge are common across many government examinations.
After Class 10
There is no compulsory stream for “Government Jobs.” Choose MPC, BiPC, CEC, Commerce or Humanities based on the jobs and backup career you may want later. If you are undecided, completing Class 12 and preferably a bachelor’s degree keeps far more government-job options open.
10th-level exams
Examples include SSC MTS / some SSC Selection Posts, Railway Level-1/Group-D-type notifications and other department-specific recruitments when announced. Qualification, age and physical requirements depend on the exact post.
12th-level exams
SSC CHSL is a major Central Government route for posts such as LDC/JSA and Data Entry roles. RRB NTPC Undergraduate is a Railway route for notified 12th-level non-technical posts. Stenographer and various department-specific recruitments are other possibilities.
Graduate-level exams
SSC CGL — major route into Group B and Group C posts across Central Government ministries and organisations.
RRB NTPC Graduate — Railway graduate roles such as Station Master, Goods Train Manager, Junior Accounts Assistant and Senior Clerk in recent notifications.
APPSC Group-I / Group-II and other APPSC recruitments — state-government pathways depending on the notification and qualification.
Technical route
ITI, Diploma, Engineering and other professional qualifications open specialised posts such as Railway ALP/Technician/JE, SSC Junior Engineer, APPSC Assistant Engineer/AEE and department-specific technical jobs. A technical qualification can therefore give you both government and private-sector options.
You must clear
There is no one fixed route.

10th: Class 10 → eligible SSC/Railway/department notification → exam/skill/physical stage as required → document verification → appointment.

12th: Class 12 → SSC CHSL / RRB NTPC UG / eligible notification → exam stages → skill/typing/medical if required → appointment.

Graduate: Bachelor’s degree → SSC CGL / APPSC Group exams / RRB NTPC Graduate / other recruitment → exam stages → document/medical/skill requirements → appointment.

Technical: ITI/Diploma/Degree → technical recruitment exam → trade/technical/medical stages as required → appointment.
ImportantDo not prepare for “all government exams” at once. First choose 2–3 exam families with similar syllabus and eligibility — for example SSC + Railways, or APPSC + SSC — then prepare systematically.
CIRCLE 3 · JOB FAMILIES

What government jobs actually exist?

Government employment includes administration, finance, railways, engineering, clerical work, field inspection, teaching, health, public safety and many specialised departments.

SSC CGL — Central Government
For graduates. Current CGL recruitments include posts such as Assistant Section Officer, Income Tax Inspector, Central Excise/Customs-related Inspector, Assistant Audit/Accounts roles, Auditor, Accountant and other Group B/C positions depending on the year.
MinistriesTaxAuditAdministration
SSC CHSL — 12th level
For eligible Class 12 candidates. Current SSC CHSL recruitment covers Group C posts such as Lower Division Clerk / Junior Secretariat Assistant and Data Entry Operator categories in Central Government offices.
SSC MTS / Selection Posts
Entry-level and post-specific Central Government recruitments. SSC Selection Post examinations can include vacancies with Matriculation, 10+2 or Graduation-level minimum qualifications depending on the individual post.
Railways — NTPC
Non-technical Railway careers. Recent graduate notifications include Station Master, Chief Commercial cum Ticket Supervisor, Goods Train Manager, Junior Account Assistant cum Typist and Senior Clerk cum Typist. Separate undergraduate NTPC notifications cover 12th-level categories.
Station MasterOperationsAccountsCommercial
Railways — Technical
Assistant Loco Pilot, Technician, Junior Engineer and specialised technical recruitments are notified separately. Eligibility can require ITI, Diploma or an engineering/technical qualification depending on the post.
APPSC Group-I
Higher-level Andhra Pradesh state administration. Group-I can include executive and administrative services depending on the notification. This is highly competitive and should be treated as a separate serious exam pathway, similar in preparation intensity to other high-level civil-service exams.
APPSC Group-II
A major Andhra Pradesh route for notified executive and non-executive state posts. The exact posts and qualifications change by notification, so do not memorise one old list and assume it will remain the same.
APPSC Specific Posts
APPSC also recruits separately for specialised positions. Recent notification pages include posts such as Agriculture Officer, Assistant Engineer, Assistant Executive Engineer, Forest Section Officer, Horticulture Officer, municipal accounts roles and welfare posts. These often require a specific degree or professional qualification.
Government Engineering
Civil, Mechanical and Electrical engineers can target SSC JE, Railways JE, APPSC AE/AEE, public works, water, power and other technical departments when vacancies are notified.
Teaching / Education
Government Teacher, Lecturer and other education careers have their own qualification and recruitment rules — such as teacher-education requirements, TET/DSC or commission/university recruitment depending on the post. Do not treat a general SSC exam as the route to every teaching job.
Public Sector / PSU
Government-owned companies recruit engineers, finance professionals, management graduates and other specialists through their own notifications. Some engineering PSUs use GATE scores; others run direct tests/interviews. PSU jobs are not the same as SSC civil-service posts.

How often do jobs come?
Not on one guaranteed schedule. SSC has an annual examination calendar, but vacancy numbers and posts vary. Railways issue separate Centralised Employment Notices. APPSC notifications depend on state vacancies and recruitment decisions. You may have periods with many notifications and periods with fewer suitable posts.
Competition
Competition can be enormous. For example, SSC stated that more than 28 lakh candidates submitted applications for CGL 2026. That does not mean all 28 lakh will appear or compete for one post, but it shows why disciplined preparation and a Plan B matter.
Scams
No genuine government recruiter can “guarantee” selection for money. Railway Recruitment Boards explicitly warn candidates to rely only on official RRB websites and beware of touts/job racketeers. Apply only through official recruitment portals.

CIRCLE 4

What will you earn?

Government pay is determined by the exact post and pay level — not simply by saying “government employee.” Basic pay is only one part; applicable DA, HRA, transport and other allowances vary.

10th / entry-level posts
Often around ₹18,000+ basic/month
Many Level-1 Central Government posts begin around this basic-pay level. Exact pay, deductions and allowances depend on the notification and posting.
12th-level clerical posts
₹19,900+ basic/month
For example, SSC CHSL currently lists LDC/JSA at Pay Level-2: ₹19,900–₹63,200. Other 12th-level posts can use different pay levels.
Railway graduate NTPC
₹29,200 – ₹35,400 initial basic/month
Recent RRB NTPC graduate notifications list ₹29,200 initial pay for posts such as Goods Train Manager/JAA/Senior Clerk and ₹35,400 for Station Master/Chief Commercial cum Ticket Supervisor.
SSC CGL graduate posts
Varies by post — up to ₹47,600 starting basic in current higher CGL levels
CGL 2026 includes multiple pay levels. Current Level-8 posts begin at ₹47,600 basic, while other CGL posts are placed in lower pay levels. Do not assume every CGL selection starts at the highest level.
AP Government
APPSC salaries depend on the specific service and post. Group-I, Group-II, engineering, agriculture, forest, welfare and clerical posts can have very different state pay scales. Always read the pay scale in the actual notification.
What comes with it?
Depending on the service/post, compensation can include applicable DA, HRA or government accommodation, travel allowances, leave and retirement benefits under prevailing rules. Transfers, probation, departmental exams and promotion rules also vary.
Do the mathsDo not spend 3–5 years preparing only because the job feels “secure.” Compare the likely salary with what you could earn using your degree in the private sector. Government preparation is strongest when you have a real backup skill or career.

How to read your four circles

Want public service + value stability — Government jobs may fit you.

Want very fast salary growth — compare strong private-sector careers too.

Only reason is “job security” — explore the actual work before committing years to exams.

One extra rule: “Government job” is too broad to be a career plan. Your plan should name the exam family and the actual roles you want.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

An 8th–10th class student does not need government-exam coaching. Your biggest advantage now is building strong fundamentals and keeping options open.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen arithmetic, reasoning, English/Telugu reading and general awareness
  • Learn basic computer skills: typing, spreadsheets, documents and internet research
  • Do not memorise current-affairs books for an exam that is still years away — understand the world instead
2After Class 10
  • Choose the stream that supports both the government jobs and private/professional career you may want
  • If possible, complete at least Class 12; a bachelor’s degree opens an even larger set of SSC, APPSC and Railway roles
  • If you prefer practical work, an ITI or Polytechnic Diploma can create strong technical government + private-sector options
3When you become eligible
  • Shortlist 2–3 exam families based on qualification and desired jobs: SSC, Railways, APPSC, technical recruitment, etc.
  • Read the official syllabus and previous papers before buying coaching
  • Track only official websites — ssc.gov.in, official RRB websites, APPSC portal and the recruiting department — and never pay anyone promising guaranteed selection
Best first testChoose three actual jobs — for example SSC Assistant Section Officer, Railway Station Master and APPSC Group-II officer. Compare their qualification, exam, work, posting, salary and promotion path. If you still prefer these jobs after understanding the work — not just the security — government service may genuinely suit you.

This is career guidance, not an official recruitment or salary notice.
