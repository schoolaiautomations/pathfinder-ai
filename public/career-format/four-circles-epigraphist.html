Epigraphist

Epigraphists study inscriptions written on stone, copper plates, temple walls, pillars, coins and other materials. They identify scripts and languages, copy inscriptions, decipher difficult letters, date them and interpret what they reveal about rulers, society, religion, economy and culture. This is a specialised research career closely connected with Archaeology, Ancient History, Palaeography and languages.

CIRCLE 1

Would Epigraphy suit you?

Epigraphy is ideal for someone who enjoys slow, careful detective work with old scripts and historical evidence.

Do you enjoy History enough to study old kingdoms, temples, inscriptions and primary sources?

Would you enjoy learning old scripts such as Brahmi, Telugu-Kannada, Grantha, Nagari or other regional scripts?

Can you patiently compare tiny differences in letters, dates and damaged words?

Would you enjoy visiting temples, monuments, caves or villages to document inscriptions in the field?

Are you interested in languages such as Telugu, Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil, Kannada or Persian/Arabic depending on your area?

CIRCLE 2

Which route can you take?

There is no single national “Epigraphist entrance exam.” The usual pathway is History / Archaeology / Ancient Indian History → Epigraphy specialisation → research or government recruitment.

Important termsEpigraphy = study of inscriptions. Palaeography = study of ancient writing and scripts. ASI = Archaeological Survey of India. An Epigraphist often needs both language knowledge and the ability to read changing scripts across centuries.
After Class 10
Humanities / CEC with History is the most directly relevant route.

Science or Commerce students can also enter History/Archaeology later if the university allows it, but choosing History early gives useful background in ancient and medieval India.
After Class 12
Good undergraduate routes include:
BA HistoryBA ArchaeologyAncient HistoryAnthropologyClassical Languages

You do not usually become an Epigraphist immediately after a bachelor's degree; specialisation normally comes later.
Postgraduate route
BA / relevant degree → MA Ancient Indian History, Culture & Archaeology / Archaeology / History → Epigraphy & Palaeography courses → research experience → Epigraphist / Archaeology research roles.

For Andhra Pradesh students, Sri Venkateswara University, Tirupati currently offers a 2-year MA in Ancient Indian History, Culture & Archaeology and a 1-year PG Diploma in Epigraphy.
What the Epigraphy course teaches
SVU's current Epigraphy & Palaeography syllabus includes Brahmi, Kharoshthi, Nagari, evolution of Telugu-Kannada scripts, inscription types, dating methods, numerals, Prakrit, Sanskrit and regional-language inscriptions.

That is much closer to real epigraphy than simply reading modern Telugu or Sanskrit.
Language specialisation
Your language choice depends on the inscriptions you want to study. South Indian epigraphy may require combinations of Telugu, Kannada, Tamil, Sanskrit and Prakrit. ASI also has a separate branch for Arabic and Persian inscriptions.

Strong reading knowledge of at least one classical/historical language can make you much more useful.
PhD route
MA → M.Phil./PhD or direct PhD route where offered → specialise in inscriptions, scripts, dynasties, temple studies, historical geography or related areas.

A PhD is especially useful for university teaching, senior research and highly specialised epigraphy posts.
Government / ASI route
The Archaeological Survey of India has a dedicated Directorate / Epigraphy Branch for Sanskrit & Dravidian inscriptions at Mysuru, along with zonal offices, and a separate Arabic & Persian inscriptions branch.

Government epigraphy posts are specialised and may be filled through direct recruitment or departmental promotion depending on the post. Eligibility can require specific Master's qualifications, language knowledge, epigraphy/archaeology experience or diplomas.
Academic route
MA → UGC-NET / SET where applicable → PhD → university teaching / research.

Epigraphy is commonly taught inside Ancient History, Archaeology, History, Culture, Sanskrit or regional-language departments rather than as a huge standalone department.
Complete roadmap
Class 10 → Humanities/CEC with History preferred → BA History / Archaeology / Ancient History → MA Ancient Indian History & Archaeology / related field → PG Diploma or strong coursework in Epigraphy & Palaeography → field documentation + language/script expertise → ASI / university / museum / research project → Epigraphist.
ImportantEpigraphy is a niche career. Do not depend on a permanent “Epigraphist” vacancy appearing immediately after graduation. Build a broader foundation in History/Archaeology and keep teaching, museums, heritage, research and archaeology as parallel career options.
CIRCLE 3 · THE WORK

What does an Epigraphist actually do?

Epigraphists turn old inscriptions into usable historical evidence through field documentation, script reading, language analysis and publication.

Discover / Locate Inscriptions
Survey temples, monuments, caves, fields, stone slabs and other locations where inscriptions may survive.
Copy the Inscription
Prepare photographs, digital records or traditional estampages so the inscription can be studied safely and accurately away from the monument.
Decipher the Script
Identify old letter forms and convert them into readable characters. The same language may look very different in scripts separated by centuries.
Transcribe
Write the inscription carefully in a standard readable form while marking damaged, missing or uncertain letters appropriately.
Translate
Translate the text into a modern scholarly language while preserving the historical meaning and technical terms.
Date the Inscription
Use regnal years, calendars, eras, palaeographic features, rulers, genealogies and historical context to estimate or confirm the date.
Interpret History
Use inscriptions to understand land grants, taxation, kings, temples, administration, trade, social groups, religion, language and local history.
Publish Research
Prepare scholarly readings, translations and notes for journals, catalogues, reports and publications such as archaeological or epigraphical series.
Digital Epigraphy
Modern projects increasingly use high-resolution photography, 3D imaging, GIS, databases, digital archives and image enhancement to document and study inscriptions.
Epigraphist vs Archaeologist
Epigraphist: specialises in inscriptions, scripts and their historical interpretation.
Archaeologist: studies material remains more broadly through survey, excavation, artefacts, architecture and scientific methods.

Epigraphy is one important speciality within the wider archaeology/history field.
Epigraphist vs Historian
Historian: may use many kinds of written and other sources to reconstruct the past.
Epigraphist: specialises in primary inscriptional evidence and the scripts/languages needed to read it.
Epigraphist vs Linguist
Linguist: studies language scientifically — structure, sound, grammar, change and usage.
Epigraphist: uses language plus script, historical context, dating and archaeology to interpret inscriptions.
Where you can work
Archaeological Survey of India, State Archaeology & Museums Departments, universities, museums, manuscript/heritage institutions, temple-history projects, research institutes and heritage documentation projects.
Current ASI work
ASI's Directorate of Epigraphy continues active surveys. Its 2025 activity reporting describes inscriptions being copied, deciphered, transcribed and prepared for publication, including field surveys in Andhra Pradesh and other states.
Andhra Pradesh advantage
Andhra Pradesh has a rich inscriptional record from ancient and medieval dynasties. Learning the evolution of Telugu-Kannada script, Sanskrit/Prakrit and regional history can be especially useful for students who want to work on South Indian epigraphy.

Career realityReading an inscription is not guessing old letters. A professional reading must be supported by script knowledge, language, chronology, comparison and historical context.
CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Epigraphy is a small specialist field. Income depends on whether you work in government archaeology, university research, museums, funded projects or heritage documentation.

Research / project assistant
₹15,000 – ₹30,000/month
Short-term archaeology/history/heritage projects can begin around this broad range depending on qualification, funding agency and project.
Academic / research fellowship
Fellowship-based during higher study
Students entering UGC/CSIR or other funded research routes may receive JRF/SRF stipends under the applicable fellowship scheme while completing PhD research.
ASI Assistant Superintending Epigraphist
Pay Level 7 · ₹44,900 basic at entry
A 2025 ASI promotion order identifies Assistant Superintending Epigraphist as a Group B Gazetted post in Level 7: ₹44,900–₹1,42,400.
ASI Deputy Superintending Epigraphist
Pay Level 10 · ₹56,100 basic at entry
ASI's 2025 seniority information for Deputy Superintending Epigraphist (Dravidian Inscriptions) lists Pay Level 10. This is a senior specialist position requiring advanced qualifications/experience, not an entry-level graduate job.
University teaching / research
Depends on academic post and institution
Assistant Professor, project-researcher and university research salaries follow the institution's recruitment rules. NET/SET/PhD requirements may apply depending on the post.
Study cost
Epigraphy does not require an expensive private degree. A strong BA/MA in History/Archaeology plus a specialised Epigraphy/Palaeography diploma or university course can be a more sensible route. Language study and field practice matter greatly.
Money warningDo not choose Epigraphy expecting a large number of job openings. It is intellectually valuable but highly specialised. Build parallel skills in Archaeology, Heritage, Museums, Languages, Teaching or Digital Humanities so you have a wider career base.

How to read your four circles

History + languages + detective-like detail — Epigraphy may be a strong fit.

Love excavations and artefacts more than inscriptions — compare Archaeology.

Love language itself more than historical inscriptions — compare Linguistics / Classical Languages.

One extra rule: an Epigraphist helps history speak by turning silent inscriptions into readable evidence.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

For an 8th–10th class student, begin with History, languages and careful observation — not with a specialised degree decision.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen History, Telugu and English; begin Sanskrit if it is available and genuinely interests you
  • Visit museums, temples and historical monuments and read the information boards carefully
  • Learn the idea of a primary source: evidence created in the historical period itself
2After Class 10
  • Prefer Humanities / CEC with History if available
  • After Class 12, compare BA History, Archaeology, Ancient History and relevant language programmes
  • Keep language learning active — especially Telugu/Sanskrit and another regional/classical language relevant to your research interest
3After Bachelor's
  • Complete an MA in Ancient Indian History & Archaeology / Archaeology / History
  • Add a specialised PG Diploma / coursework in Epigraphy & Palaeography; SVU Tirupati is one current AP example
  • Build field experience by documenting inscriptions, learning estampage/digital recording methods and working with research/heritage projects
Best first testVisit a historical temple or monument with a published inscription. Photograph only where permitted, then compare the inscription with an authoritative published transcription. Try to identify repeated characters and understand how scholars dated it. If the script itself becomes as interesting as the monument, Epigraphy may genuinely suit you.
