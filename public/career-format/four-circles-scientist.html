Scientist / Researcher

Scientists ask questions that nobody has fully answered yet. They design experiments, collect evidence, analyse data and build new knowledge — in fields ranging from Physics and Biology to Space, Materials, Agriculture, Medicine and Artificial Intelligence. The work can be exciting, but it also means failed experiments, long study, careful writing and patience.

CIRCLE 1

Do you really want research?

Do not choose “Scientist” only because ISRO rockets or laboratories look exciting. Real research is often slow, uncertain and full of repetition.

When you learn a fact in Science, do you often ask “Why does this happen?” or “How do we know this is true?”?

Can you repeat an experiment or analysis many times when the first result is unclear or wrong?

Do you enjoy reading deeply about one topic rather than only learning enough to pass an exam?

Can you accept that a good scientific result may be “our idea was wrong”?

Are you comfortable with Maths, data, graphs, laboratory work or coding depending on your scientific field?

Would you still choose science if becoming an independent researcher required 5–10 years of higher study after Class 12?

CIRCLE 2

Subjects & pathway

There is no single “Scientist entrance exam.” The route depends on what science you want to study.

1Science fundamentals
Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Mathematics are the foundations. You do not need to love all four equally, but strong scientists understand the basics around their speciality.
2Maths & Statistics
Data, probability, graphs, modelling and measurement are central to modern research. Even many Biology and Medicine researchers use statistics and coding.
3English & Writing
Scientists read research papers, write reports, publish results, present at conferences and explain evidence. Clear writing is a scientific skill.
After Class 10
MPC is the strongest route for Physics, Mathematics, Astronomy, Computer/AI, many Space and Materials fields.
BiPC is strong for Biology, Life Sciences, Biotechnology, Medical and many Agricultural research fields.
PCMB / Maths + Biology, where available and manageable, keeps the widest science options open. The best stream depends on the scientific field you want.
Direct research-oriented routes
IISER Aptitude Test (IAT) — major route into 5-year BS-MS dual-degree science programmes at IISERs. Current IAT 2026 rules require at least three subjects among Biology, Chemistry, Mathematics and Physics for the standard BS-MS science route.

NEST — entrance to the 5-year Integrated M.Sc. programmes at NISER Bhubaneswar and UM-DAE CEBS Mumbai.

IISc BS (Research) — in 2026, Indian applicants are selected using JEE Advanced 2026 or IAT 2026; IISc currently requires Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics at Class 12 level.
Regular university route
Class 12 → B.Sc. / BS in Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology, Biotechnology, Geology, Statistics or another science → M.Sc. / specialised master's → research / PhD. Admission may be through CUET-UG, state/university admission, merit or an institution-specific entrance process.
After B.Sc. / BS
IIT JAM is one major postgraduate route. JAM 2026 covers Biotechnology, Chemistry, Geology, Mathematics, Mathematical Statistics and Physics among its science papers, with postgraduate programmes across IITs and result-sharing institutions.

Other universities and institutes may have their own M.Sc., Integrated PhD or research admission routes.
PhD / research stage
For many independent research-scientist, university and advanced R&D careers, a PhD is common and often required or strongly preferred. Depending on the field, entry can involve CSIR-UGC NET/JRF, GATE, institute exams/interviews or other national fellowships. The exact requirement changes by programme.
Engineering route
You can also become a scientist through Engineering. ISRO and DRDO, for example, recruit Scientist/Engineer or Scientist positions in engineering and technology disciplines. A student may follow MPC → B.Tech → GATE / direct recruitment / M.Tech / PhD → R&D scientist or scientist-engineer role.
You must clear
Pure-science route: Class 12 science → IAT / NEST / university admission → BS-MS / Integrated M.Sc. OR B.Sc./BS → M.Sc. / research training → PhD for many advanced research careers → Scientist / Researcher.

Engineering-scientist route: MPC → B.Tech → R&D recruitment / M.Tech / PhD depending on role → Scientist / Scientist-Engineer.
ImportantB.Sc. does not automatically make someone a “Scientist,” and PhD is not compulsory for every job with the word Scientist. Qualification depends heavily on the organisation and role. Always read the exact recruitment notice.
CIRCLE 3 · SCIENCE FIELDS

What kind of scientist could you become?

“Scientist” becomes meaningful only when you add the field. These careers can feel completely different from one another.

Physics
Study matter, energy, forces and the laws of nature.
Fields: condensed matter, optics, photonics, quantum science, particle physics, nuclear physics, instrumentation.
Workplaces: universities, IISc/IISER/NISER, national labs, DAE/CSIR institutions.
QuantumOpticsNuclear
Chemistry
Create and understand molecules and materials.
Fields: organic, inorganic, physical, analytical, medicinal, catalysis and materials chemistry.
Workplaces: pharma, chemicals, CSIR labs, universities, batteries and materials companies.
MoleculesDrugsMaterials
Biology / Life Sciences
Study living systems.
Fields: molecular biology, genetics, microbiology, cell biology, ecology, neuroscience and developmental biology.
Workplaces: research institutes, biotech, pharma, universities, conservation and health research.
GenesCellsMicrobes
Biotechnology
Use biology to create useful products and processes.
Fields: vaccines, biologics, fermentation, diagnostics, genomics, synthetic biology and bioprocess research.
Workplaces: biotech/pharma companies, research institutes and start-ups.
Space Science
Study space or develop space technology.
Fields: planetary science, atmospheric science, remote sensing, astrophysics, satellite science, propulsion and spacecraft systems.
Workplaces: ISRO centres, universities, astronomy institutes and space companies.
ISROSatellitesPlanetary
Astronomy / Astrophysics
Study stars, galaxies, black holes, cosmology and the Universe using Physics, Mathematics, telescopes, simulations and large datasets. Higher study — usually through Physics/Astronomy followed by postgraduate research — is the normal route.
Defence Research
Develop technology for national defence.
Fields: missiles, radar, electronics, materials, AI, aeronautics, combat systems, sensors and life sciences.
Workplaces: DRDO laboratories and defence R&D organisations.
Materials Scientist
Study and invent materials with better properties. Batteries, semiconductors, metals, polymers, ceramics, composites and nanomaterials.
Workplaces: EV/battery companies, semiconductor firms, aerospace, steel, universities and national labs.
Medical / Biomedical Research
Investigate disease, diagnostics, drugs, public health and human biology. Researchers may come from Biology, Biotechnology, Pharmacy, Medicine, Biomedical Engineering or related backgrounds.
Workplaces: ICMR institutes, medical colleges, hospitals, pharma and biotech.
Agricultural Scientist
Improve crops, soil, livestock, pests, food and farming systems.
Fields: plant breeding, genetics, agronomy, soil science, entomology, horticulture and agricultural biotechnology.
Workplaces: ICAR institutes, agricultural universities, seed/agri-tech companies.
Environmental / Climate
Study climate, pollution, ecosystems, water and Earth systems.
Fields: climate modelling, atmospheric science, oceanography, ecology, environmental chemistry and hydrology.
Earth / Geological Science
Study rocks, minerals, earthquakes, groundwater, petroleum systems, natural hazards and Earth's history. Geology and Earth Sciences can lead to universities, surveys, energy/mining and environmental research.
Computer / AI Research
Invent new algorithms and computing methods.
Fields: AI, machine learning, robotics, computer vision, language models, cybersecurity, algorithms and human-computer interaction.
Route: CSE/Maths/Statistics/Engineering → advanced research experience → often M.Tech/MS/PhD for research-heavy roles.
Nuclear / Atomic Science
Work in nuclear physics, reactors, radiation science, materials and atomic-energy applications. DAE institutions and BARC are major Indian ecosystems; recruitment pathways depend on the exact scientific or engineering discipline.
Ocean Scientist
Study oceans, marine systems, climate, coastal processes and underwater resources using oceanography, Earth science, biology, chemistry and engineering.
Scientist vs Engineer
Scientist: mainly asks “What is true? Why does this happen? Can we discover something new?”
Engineer: mainly asks “How can we design something that works reliably under real constraints?”
Reality: they overlap heavily. ISRO even uses the title Scientist/Engineer because advanced technology requires both.
Scientist vs Lab Technician
Scientist: usually designs studies, interprets results and develops new knowledge.
Technician / Technical Assistant: operates, maintains or supports laboratory and technical systems. Both are valuable careers, but the qualification and responsibility are different.
Where scientists work
ISRO, DRDO, CSIR laboratories, DAE/BARC, ICMR, ICAR, IISc, IISERs, NISER, IITs, universities, pharma, biotech, chemicals, batteries, semiconductor, aerospace, agri-tech, AI companies and research start-ups.

CIRCLE 4

What you may earn

Science has several earning stages. A PhD fellowship is training support, not the same thing as a permanent scientist salary.

B.Sc. / early lab role
₹20,000 – ₹35,000/month
Lab assistants, project staff, QC/QA and junior research-support roles can fall around this range, but qualification and industry matter greatly. Many research-intensive scientist jobs require further study.
JRF — research fellowship
₹37,000/month stipend
CSIR's current research-scheme rate lists ₹37,000/month for JRF during the first two years, subject to the scheme's eligibility and conditions. This is a research fellowship, not a normal permanent salary.
SRF
₹42,000/month stipend
Current CSIR research-scheme guidance lists ₹42,000/month for SRF / upgraded fellowship stages where applicable.
Research Associate
₹58,000 – ₹67,000/month
CSIR currently lists Research Associate consolidated emolument slabs of ₹58,000, ₹61,000 or ₹67,000 depending on qualification and experience.
Government Scientist — entry example
₹56,100 basic/month + allowances
DRDO Scientist B currently begins at Pay Level 10 with ₹56,100 initial basic pay. ISRO Scientist/Engineer 'SC' is also commonly Level 10, ₹56,100–₹1,77,500, depending on recruitment discipline and notification.
Experienced scientist
₹70,000 – ₹1,50,000+/month
Experienced government, private R&D, pharma, biotech, materials, semiconductor and AI researchers can move into this broad range or above. Senior scientific leadership can earn substantially more.
Study cost & support
Science education can be relatively affordable at strong public institutions, and some research-oriented programmes offer scholarships. For example, NEST states that students admitted to NISER/UM-DAE CEBS can be eligible for a ₹60,000 annual DISHA scholarship plus ₹20,000 per year for summer internships. Scholarship rules can change and should be checked in the admission year.
Think long-termIf your family needs you to earn immediately after Class 12 or a bachelor's degree, understand that the classic research-scientist pathway may involve M.Sc./Integrated Master's + PhD before reaching stable independent research positions. Build employable laboratory, coding, data or technical skills along the way.

How to read your four circles

Love asking questions + patient with uncertainty — research may be a strong fit.

Love science but prefer building products — Engineering, Medicine, Pharmacy or applied technology may fit better.

Love science but not long research study — explore laboratory, quality, technical, data or science-communication careers.

One extra rule: a scientist is not someone who knows every answer. A scientist is someone trained to find better evidence.

YOUR ACTION PLAN

What to do from today

An 8th–10th class student does not need to choose a PhD topic. First find the type of questions you enjoy investigating.

1Right now, in school
  • Strengthen Science and Maths fundamentals and ask “why?” instead of memorising only textbook answers
  • Do one small experiment or data project each month and write the question → method → result → conclusion
  • Read about scientists in different fields — not only famous physicists or space scientists
2After Class 10
  • Choose MPC for Physics/Maths/Space/AI-heavy paths, BiPC for Biology/Life Science/Medical-heavy paths, or a suitable combination if you genuinely want both
  • If targeting research-focused institutes, explore IAT, NEST and IISc's current BS Research admission routes
  • Learn basic Python/data analysis or laboratory measurement — modern scientists increasingly need quantitative skills
3During college
  • Do research internships and work with professors before deciding whether a PhD genuinely suits you
  • Read actual research papers, present posters and learn statistics/coding/lab techniques relevant to your field
  • After B.Sc./BS, compare M.Sc., Integrated PhD, JAM, CSIR-NET/JRF, GATE and institute-specific research routes based on your speciality
Best first testChoose a question you genuinely do not know the answer to — for example, “Does water temperature affect how fast sugar dissolves?” Design a fair experiment, collect measurements, make a graph and explain what the evidence says. If the investigation is more exciting than simply being told the answer, you may have a research mindset.
